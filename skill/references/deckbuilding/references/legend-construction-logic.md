# Legend construction logic (catalog)

This file exists because the method demonstrated on Kai'Sa and Annie in the main deckbuilding book scales cleanly to the full roster -- the derivation is grounded in permanent card text and the game's own already-established archetype framework, not a community-guide snapshot, so it doesn't carry the staleness risk that would come from writing up 46 entries' worth of "what people currently play." What would go stale (a specific named supporting card, a specific tournament decklist) simply isn't in this file -- only the structural reading of each Legend's own printed kit, and what that kit is built to do, is. Read the main book's methodology section first; this is the reference table it produces when run across the whole current roster, not a substitute for understanding the method itself.

### Ahri - Nine-Tailed Fox (Calm+Mind)

Legend ability: `When an enemy unit attacks a battlefield you control, give it -1 Might this turn, to a minimum of 1 Might.`
Ahri, Inquisitive (Mind): `When I attack or defend, give an enemy unit here -2 Might this turn, to a minimum of 1 Might.`
Ahri - Alluring (Calm): `When I hold, you score 1 point.`

**Archetype:** Control. The Legend ability only fires reactively, when an enemy attacks a battlefield you already control — it's a defensive stat-tax, not a proactive threat, and it structurally assumes you're the one sitting on ground the opponent has to come dislodge. Alluring converts that sitting-still posture directly into points: `When I hold, you score 1 point` is the single most literal "holding is the win condition" line in this batch — no conquer step needed, just still being there. Inquisitive stacks a second, larger debuff (-2 might) onto the same defensive shape. Nothing here rewards moving first or racing; everything rewards surviving the attack that comes to you.

**Core unfolding:** Early game is about getting units onto Battlefields you intend to keep, since the Legend ability is worthless until you already control ground worth defending — there's no early-aggression payoff to chase. Once a board position is established, every enemy attack into your held battlefield eats a -1 might penalty from the Legend before combat math even starts, and if Inquisitive is present, a further -2 on top when she's involved — stacking debuffs make your existing defenders survive fights they'd otherwise lose, which is what lets the position keep being "held" turn after turn. With Alluring in the Champion slot, each of those successfully-defended holds is a direct point, so the deck doesn't need a big finishing turn at all — it just needs to keep not losing the ground it already has. (Calm's stated design intent, "the peace of holding," maps onto Alluring's text about as literally as the Domain table gets.)

**Win condition:** Holding uncontested (or successfully defended) battlefields converts straight into points via Alluring — this is a hold-to-score plan, not a combat-attrition or resource-engine one. The might-debuffs exist purely to make holding survivable against attacks, not to win Showdowns outright for their own sake.

### Akali - Rogue Assassin (Fury+Calm)

Legend ability: `Empower 3 Energy, a rainbow rune (...) Action: Exhaust: If it's your turn, move a friendly unit in a showdown to base and if I'm Empowered, ready it.`
Akali, Deadly Weapon (Fury): `Empower 2 Energy, a Fury rune. When I move, you may deal 1 to a unit at a battlefield I moved to or from. If I'm Empowered, deal 2 instead. Empowered: I have +1 Might.`
Akali, Silent (Calm): `I can't be chosen by enemy spells and abilities unless I'm in combat. When I move to a battlefield, give me +2 Might this turn.`

**Archetype:** Aggro, with a skirmishing/hit-and-run texture rather than a flat board-flood. The Legend ability lets you pull a unit out of a Showdown back to base — on its face a defensive escape valve — but once Empowered it also readies that unit, turning a retreat into a free extra action rather than just damage avoidance. Both Champions key off movement itself as the trigger: Deadly Weapon pings a unit for 1-2 damage every time it moves, Silent gets +2 might every time it moves to a battlefield. None of this rewards patience or holding; all of it rewards repeatedly repositioning to force new, favorable fights.

**Core unfolding:** Early game wants Akali moving between battlefields as soon as she's down, since both Champion payoffs trigger off movement rather than a turn-count or resource threshold — even a single early move nets chip damage (Deadly Weapon) or a temporary stat spike (Silent) that can decide a close Showdown. Once the Empower cost is paid, the Legend ability upgrades from "dodge a losing fight" to "dodge a losing fight and get the unit back for another action" — meaning a unit can contest one battlefield, retreat before a bad trade resolves, and be ready to threaten a different battlefield the same cycle. That loop is what the deck is building toward: not one big swing, but a constant churn of winnable skirmishes across multiple battlefields, each one topped up by move-triggered damage or stat gain.

**Win condition:** Wins Showdowns outright through move-triggered pings and stat bumps, then uses the Empowered retreat to avoid losing units in fights it doesn't like — there's no hold-based scoring text anywhere in this kit, so the plan is pure conquest via favorable, repeatable combat, not attrition or resource accumulation.

### Ambessa - Matriarch of War (Body+Order)

Legend ability: `When you empower something else, empower me. (I become Empowered if I'm not already.) Disempower me, a rainbow rune, Exhaust: Ready a unit.`
Ambessa, The Wolf (Body): `Empower 3 Energy, a Body rune. Empowered: I have +3 Might and can't be dealt damage unless I'm in combat.`
Ambessa, Respected and Feared (Order): `Empower 1 Energy, two Order runes. Empowered: I have Assault 2. Empowered: When I attack, kill an enemy unit here with less Might than me.`

**Archetype:** Aggro. Both Champions are themselves Empower payoffs that turn a paid-up-front investment into a hard-to-answer attacker: Feared gets Assault 2 plus an on-attack execute against anything smaller, Wolf gets +3 might and immunity to non-combat removal. Empowering either one also empowers the Legend (a free chain), which can then be spent to ready a separate unit — an extra attacker or blocker on demand. Nothing here is reactive or held in reserve; the whole kit is about paying a cost this turn to push through combat this turn or next.

**Core unfolding:** Early game wants the chosen Champion down and Empowered as soon as resources allow, since the payoff is immediate combat dominance rather than a delayed engine — Feared's on-attack kill clears blockers outright, Wolf's damage immunity outside combat makes him nearly impossible to remove before he swings. Once that first Empower lands, it auto-triggers the Legend's own Empower, which can be cashed in to ready a second unit — effectively banking an extra attack or defense each turn once the chain is running, so board presence compounds turn-over-turn rather than needing a single big payoff. Closing stages look like repeatedly attacking into blockers Feared can simply kill, or trading into a Wolf the opponent can't answer outside of combat, backed by an extra readied unit most turns.

**Win condition:** Wins Showdowns outright via stat and removal advantage — killing smaller blockers on attack, or fielding a near-unkillable body — this is board dominance converting directly into conquest, not a holding or card-advantage plan.

### Azir - Emperor of the Sands (Calm+Order)

Legend ability: `Your Sand Soldiers have Weaponmaster. 1 Energy, Exhaust: Play a 2 Might Sand Soldier unit token to your base. Use only if you've played an Equipment this turn.`
Azir - Ascendant (Calm): `A Calm rune: Action — Choose a unit you control. Move me to its location and it to my original location. If it's equipped, you may attach one of its Equipment to me. Use only once per turn.`
Azir - Sovereign (Order): `Accelerate (...) When I attack, you may move any number of your token units to this battlefield.`

**Archetype:** The two Champions pull in genuinely different directions and that split is worth stating plainly rather than forcing a single label. The Legend ability is a repeatable, condition-gated engine — every turn you play an Equipment, you can spend energy to add another 2-might Sand Soldier token — which builds a wide board gradually over many turns, not in one burst. Sovereign then supplies a combo-shaped payoff: `When I attack, you may move any number of your token units to this battlefield` collapses a slowly-accumulated, dispersed token army onto a single Showdown, converting patient setup into one decisive swing. Ascendant, by contrast, is a positional utility tool — swap places with a unit and optionally steal its Equipment — that supports steadier, incremental equipment value and protecting key pieces rather than any single climactic turn; it reads as control/grind-adjacent, with no scoring text of its own.

**Core unfolding:** Early game requires playing an Equipment every turn just to keep the token engine online at all — that's a hard gate, not a suggestion, since the token-play ability explicitly does nothing without an Equipment played that same turn. With Sovereign, the deck spends several turns quietly building a wide, individually unthreatening board of 2-might tokens across multiple battlefields, then uses Sovereign's attack trigger to mass-mobilize all of them onto one contested battlefield at once, turning an otherwise unremarkable token spread into an overwhelming single Showdown. With Ascendant instead, the same token/Equipment base is used more defensively — repositioning to dodge removal aimed at a key equipped unit and consolidating Equipment onto Azir himself.

**Win condition:** With Sovereign, this is a genuine combo line: many turns of quiet token accumulation cashed in on one attack to win a Showdown outright through sudden numbers. With Ascendant, the text supports protecting equipped value and steady board presence, but nothing in her line states a direct scoring mechanism, so her contribution to actually converting into points is the weaker-supported half of this Legend's kit and shouldn't be overstated.

### Darius - Hand of Noxus (Fury+Order)

Legend ability: `Exhaust: Reaction, Legion — Add 1 Energy. (Abilities that add resources can't be reacted to. Get the effect if you've played a card this turn.)`
Darius - Trifarian (Fury): `When you play your second card in a turn, give me +2 Might this turn and ready me.`
Darius - Executioner (Order): `Legion — When you play me, ready me. (Get the effect if you've played another card this turn) Other friendly units have +1 Might here.`

**Archetype:** Aggro. All three pieces of this kit key off the same immediate, same-turn condition — playing a second card in a turn (Legion) — and every payoff resolves instantly, not on a delay: extra energy (Legend), a might buff plus a free ready (Trifarian), or entering combat-ready plus an anthem (Executioner). There's no reactive gate, no trash requirement, no end-of-turn timer — this is a deck built to front-load as many plays into a single turn as possible and cash that in immediately.

**Core unfolding:** Early game wants a curve stacked with cheap, efficient second plays so that Legion triggers reliably from turn 1 or 2 onward — the Legend's extra energy directly helps fund that second card in the first place, making the loop partially self-sustaining. Once it's online, hitting Legion in a turn gives Trifarian +2 might and readies him, meaning he can attack, come back up, and threaten to attack or defend again the same cycle; Executioner instead enters already ready and buffs every friendly unit at his battlefield by +1 might, stacking board-wide pressure at a single point of contest. Both scale purely with how consistently the deck can hit two-plus cards a turn, not with any resource that needs to build up over many turns.

**Win condition:** Wins Showdowns outright through repeated readies and stat pushes — there's no hold-scoring or card-engine text anywhere in this kit, so the entire plan is forcing and winning as many individual Showdowns as possible, turn after turn, via consistently overloaded turns.

### Diana - Scorn of the Moon (Mind+Chaos)

Legend ability: `Reaction: Exhaust: Add 1 Energy. Spend this Energy only during showdowns. (Abilities that add resources can't be reacted to.)`
Diana - Lunari (Mind): `When a showdown begins here, you may pay 1 Energy. If you do, Predict, then reveal the top card of your Main Deck. If it's a spell, draw it.`
Diana - No Longer Human (Chaos): `Ambush (...) When you play a spell, give me +2 Might this turn.`

**Archetype:** Control-leaning reactive tempo. The Legend generates energy that can only be spent during Showdowns, and No Longer Human's Ambush lets her be played reactively into a battlefield that's already being contested — neither piece wants to commit proactively ahead of time; both wait for the opponent to initiate combat and then answer it. That's a fundamentally different shape from forcing early decisions: this deck is content to let a fight come to it, provided it has saved-up resources to spend the moment it does.

**Core unfolding:** Early game means holding back spells and energy rather than dumping them, since the showdown-only energy pool is dead outside combat and No Longer Human is structurally meant to enter during an already-started fight rather than proactively. Once a Showdown begins, Lunari's trigger turns that fight into a card-advantage opportunity — paying 1 energy to preview the top card and draw it if it's a spell — so repeated Showdowns gradually accumulate resources rather than any single fight deciding the game. No Longer Human then converts spent spells directly into a same-turn might swing right as she ambushes in, letting saved-up reaction energy and Lunari's card advantage fund a favorable fight in the moment it matters. (Chaos's stated weakness — can't hold a permanent unit advantage — fits this reactive-rather-than-entrenched shape: the deck answers fights as they come rather than sitting on fixed board superiority.)

**Win condition:** Primarily wins Showdowns outright through reactive combat tricks funded by saved showdown-energy and spell-triggered might swings; Lunari's draw is a secondary resource-advantage engine that indirectly supplies more of those same combat wins over a longer game, rather than a hold-based or direct-scoring mechanism.

### Draven - Glorious Executioner (Fury+Chaos)

Legend ability: `When you win a combat, draw 1. (You win if only your units remain after combat.)`
Draven - Vanquisher (Fury): `When I win a combat, play a Gold gear token exhausted. When I attack or defend, you may pay a Fury rune. If you do, give me +2 Might this turn.`
Draven - Audacious (Chaos): `Deflect (...) The first time I win a combat each turn, you score 1 point. When I die in combat, choose an opponent. They score 1 point.`

**Archetype:** Aggro, and about as literally as this game's text gets. Every trigger in this kit — Legend, Vanquisher, Audacious alike — fires immediately off "when you win a combat," with no delay, no trash condition, no end-of-turn timer. This is a deck whose entire identity is picking fights and winning them, repeatedly, as fast as possible.

**Core unfolding:** Early game wants Draven into combat quickly and often, since every combat win refills the hand via the Legend ability, keeping resources flowing to fuel the next fight rather than needing any setup turn. With Audacious equipped, the first combat win each turn scores a point directly and immediately — sidestepping the usual conquer-then-hold cycle entirely, so the deck doesn't need to hold ground across turns, just win one fight per turn. With Vanquisher instead, each combat win reinvests into a Gold gear token and a rune-fueled might pump, making Draven progressively stickier and more likely to win the next fight too, snowballing combat dominance rather than cashing straight into points. Audacious's downside — dying in combat hands the opponent a point — means this plan wants favorable fights specifically, not just any fights; it's aggressive but not indiscriminate.

**Win condition:** With Audacious, combat wins convert directly into points on the spot, one of the most literal "wins Showdowns outright and that is the score" kits in the pool. With Vanquisher, combat wins instead fund card and stat snowballing that lets Draven keep winning subsequent fights, an indirect but still combat-only route to conquering (not holding) battlefields.

### Ezreal - Prodigal Explorer (Mind+Chaos)

Legend ability: `Exhaust: Reaction — Draw 1. Use only if you've chosen enemy units and/or gear twice this turn with spells or unit abilities.` Champions: **Ezreal, Prodigy** (Chaos) — `When you play me, discard 1, then draw 2. Optional additional costs you pay cost 1 Energy or a rainbow rune less.` **Ezreal - Dashing** (Mind) — `When I attack or defend, deal damage equal to my Might to an enemy unit here. I don't deal combat damage. A Mind rune: Action — Move me to your base.`

The Legend ability is a Reaction — it can fire on the opponent's turn — but it's gated behind a counting condition: two separate enemy-unit/gear-targeting effects in the same turn. That timing/condition pair is control's signature: hold up interaction, react rather than commit, and get paid in cards for doing your job rather than for tempo. Dashing reinforces this directly — he deals no combat damage of his own, only damage-equal-to-Might on attack or defend, making him a repeatable "choose an enemy unit" trigger rather than a stat-stick, and his Mind rune ability lets him retreat to base after triggering it, so he can proc removal and disengage rather than trade. Prodigy's discard-2-draw-2 filters the hand and its cost discount on optional additional costs broadly enables paying extra for effects (including, plausibly, more targeting effects) cheaper.

Early game wants cheap, repeatable ways to "choose" enemy units or gear — Dashing alone can supply one leg of the two-target requirement every combat. Once the engine is running, chaining two such interactions in a turn converts denial into card draw, refueling the hand while thinning the opponent's board rather than building a stat lead of its own. The close-out isn't an explosive turn — it's attrition: repeated removal proc'd for free card advantage, until the opponent no longer has enough board to contest Showdowns. The engine converts into points indirectly, through resource advantage — Ezreal's kit doesn't generate a win-Showdown-outright combat stat or a holding aura, it generates the draws that let you keep removing what the opponent needs to attack or defend with.

### Fiora - Grand Duelist (Body+Order)

Legend ability: `When one of your units becomes Mighty, you may exhaust me to channel 1 rune exhausted. (A unit is Mighty while it has 5+ Might.)` Champions: **Fiora - Peerless** (Body) — `When I attack or defend one on one, double my Might this combat.` **Fiora - Worthy** (Order) — `When a unit you control becomes Mighty, you may pay an Order rune to ready it.`

The Legend ability doesn't fire on a fixed timing — it fires on a state change, "becomes Mighty," meaning it's dormant until a unit crosses the 5+ Might line, then pays out a rune. That's a threshold-crossing trigger, and both Champions are built to make crossings happen repeatedly and matter more when they do. Peerless doubles her own Might in a 1v1 combat — a single Showdown can push her from below 5 to comfortably above it mid-fight, meaning the "becomes Mighty" event can happen inside combat itself rather than needing pre-buffing. Worthy converts every such crossing into a readied unit — an extra action, not just a resource.

That combination — an in-combat stat-double that can trip the threshold, paired with a Champion that turns each trip into an untapped unit — is a combo shape: the deck wants to set up a 1v1 Showdown where Fiora crosses 5+ Might, which simultaneously channels a rune (Legend) and readies her (Worthy), letting her act again — attack elsewhere, defend, or simply be available for the next threshold event — inside the same turn. This isn't aggro's board-flood (nothing here rewards adding bodies) and it isn't control's slow denial (nothing here removes or holds space passively); it's a patient build toward a specific stat line that, once hit, cascades into extra actions. Early game is about getting one or two units to the doorstep of 5 Might cheaply — Peerless's own doubling is the cheapest route, needing only a 1v1 fight rather than external buffs. Once running, each Mighty crossing both ramps resources (rune channel) and re-enables combat (Worthy's ready), which converts directly into winning Showdowns outright — a readied Fiora can contest another battlefield or defend the one she just took, so the payoff is repeated combat wins funded by her own stat growth, not a resource stockpile cashed in later.

### Garen - Might of Demacia (Body+Order)

Legend ability: `When you conquer, if you have 4+ units at that battlefield, draw 2.` Champions: **Garen - Rugged** (Body) — `Assault 2, Shield 2 (+2 Might while I'm an attacker or defender).` **Garen - Commander** (Order) — `Other friendly units have +1 Might here.`

The trigger is immediate and on-the-spot — the moment you conquer, not end of turn, not reactive — but it's gated on a hard board-width requirement: 4+ units already stacked at that one battlefield. That's an aggro condition in the most literal sense the framework describes: force a single battlefield to a numbers head before the opponent can match it, and get burst-refueled (draw 2) for succeeding. Commander's aura directly supports the pile-up, buffing every other friendly unit at that same battlefield — so going wide at one location isn't just for the draw trigger, it's also how the stack actually wins the fight. Rugged makes Garen himself efficient in both directions (Assault 2 attacking, Shield 2 defending), so he can lead the initial push and then help the stacked board hold the location afterward rather than being a one-way beater.

Early game wants multiple cheap units converging on the same battlefield rather than spread across three — the payoff specifically needs 4+ bodies at the location being conquered, so width concentrated in one place matters more than width in general. Once the first stacked conquest lands, the draw-2 refuels the hand for the next wave, letting the deck keep re-flooding a battlefield rather than running out of gas after one push — the classic wide-aggro sustain loop. The close is direct: Commander's buffed stack and Rugged's Shield 2 aren't just for winning the initial Showdown, they're what lets the same pile of units hold the battlefield against the counter-push, so this Legend converts into points by winning Showdowns outright through numeric and stat superiority, then holding with the same bodies.

### Irelia - Blade Dancer (Calm+Chaos)

Legend ability: `When you choose a friendly unit, you may exhaust me and pay a rainbow rune to ready it. When you conquer, you may pay 1 Energy to ready me.` Champions: **Irelia - Fervent** (Calm) — `Deflect. When you choose or ready me, give me +1 Might this turn.` **Irelia - Graceful** (Chaos) — `Your spells that choose me cost 1 Energy or a rainbow rune less.`

The Legend ability is reactive to your own targeting — any time you "choose" (target) a friendly unit, you can pay to ready it. That converts self-targeting spells/abilities into extra actions rather than being a fixed-timing payoff, and it stacks with a second clause readying Irelia herself on every conquest. Fervent turns each choose-or-ready event into a stat gain (+1 Might), so repeatedly targeting her both readies her (Legend) and grows her (Fervent) in the same motion. Graceful discounts spells that choose her specifically, which is the enabler that makes chaining several such triggers in one turn actually affordable.

Put together, this is a combo-leaning identity: the payoff scales with how many "choose my own unit" effects you can chain onto one body in a single turn, not with how many units you field. A turn built around Graceful's discount lets you fire multiple self-targeting spells at Fervent, each one readying her (via the Legend) and pumping her Might (via Fervent), potentially letting her attack, defend, or move more than once before the turn ends — an outsized single-turn swing from a small number of cards rather than a wide board or a long grind. Early game wants cheap friendly-unit-targeting spells in the list specifically so there's fuel for the loop; once it's running, the loop's readying directly re-enables combat actions rather than just banking value for later. That makes the win condition direct: repeated readying lets the same unit contest or re-contest Showdowns multiple times in one turn, converting the targeting loop into outright Showdown wins rather than a resource stockpile spent elsewhere.

### Ivern - Green Father (Calm+Order)

Legend ability: `When you conquer or hold, you may exhaust me to replace that battlefield with a Brush battlefield token. (Bird, Cat, Dog, Poro, and Ivern units have +1 Might in Brush. It can be swapped back when scored.)` Champions: **Ivern - Nurturer** (Calm) — `When you play me or when I hold, look at the top 3 cards of your Main Deck. You may reveal a unit from among them and draw it. Recycle the rest. Then if you revealed a Bird, Cat, Dog, or Poro, buff a friendly unit.` **Ivern - Friend to All** (Order) — `As you play me, choose Bird, Cat, Dog, or Poro. I gain that tag. When I conquer or hold, score 1 point if your units have all of the following tags among them — Bird, Cat, Dog, and Poro.`

The Legend ability fires on conquer or hold — notably including hold, the passive "still there next turn" state, not just the active moment of taking a battlefield — and converts that territory into permanently upgraded ground (Brush) for a specific tag family. That's a control-shaped trigger: it rewards sustained possession, not a single burst of aggression. Nurturer reinforces the patience — it digs on play and again every time you hold, filtering toward a unit and conditionally buffing if the found card carries one of the four tags, which is consistency support for a tag-diverse build rather than raw stats. Friend to All is the sharpest structural signal: it needs all four tags — Bird, Cat, Dog, and Poro — present among your units simultaneously before it scores a point on conquer or hold. That's a genuine combo-style assembly requirement layered on top of a control-style holding shell: you must collect a specific, varied board composition before the bonus point fires, and it won't fire on turn one no matter how aggressively you play.

Early game is spent using Nurturer's dig to find and deploy tagged units, building toward having one of each of the four tags on board. Once a battlefield is being held, the Legend's Brush conversion permanently buffs whichever tagged units sit there, and if the four-tag set is complete, Friend to All adds a bonus point on top of the hold itself. The close-out is not a race — it's an accumulating value engine on top of already-held ground. The win condition is primarily indirect-to-direct via holding: Brush's stat buff helps the units already sitting on a battlefield keep winning or surviving Showdowns there over time, with Friend to All layering a secondary, conditional direct point once the tag collection is assembled — resource/composition-driven, not a burst.

### Jax - Grandmaster At Arms (Calm+Body)

Legend ability: `1 Energy, Exhaust: Attach a detached Equipment you control to a unit you control. Exhaust: Attach an attached Equipment you control to a unit you control.` Champions: **Jax - Unmatched** (Calm) — `Deflect. Each Equipment in your hand has Quick-Draw. (It gains Reaction. When you play it, attach it to a unit you control.)` **Jax - Unrelenting** (Body) — `Weaponmaster. When you attach an Equipment to me, you may pay 1 Energy to draw 1.`

The Legend ability is a plain repeatable activated ability — no opponent-dependent condition, no burst window — that redeploys Equipment between units at will. That's a flexibility/resilience tool rather than a race or setup-payoff mechanic: whichever unit currently needs the gear (about to fight, about to move, about to die) can have it moved there. Unmatched pushes this toward genuine control tempo by giving all Equipment in hand Reaction speed to attach — you can hold gear back and drop it in response to what the opponent does, rather than pre-committing and telegraphing the play. Unrelenting instead rewards re-attaching specifically onto himself, drawing a card each time — which synergizes directly with the Legend's own reattachment loop: move gear onto Unrelenting repeatedly, draw repeatedly.

This reads as a control-leaning value engine built around a durable, gear-carrying unit rather than either an aggro flood or a combo detonation — nothing here rewards board width or a specific assembled state, and nothing pays off in one explosive turn. Early game wants Equipment landed cheaply (Unrelenting's Weaponmaster discount on entry). Once running, the Legend ability keeps redirecting that gear to whichever unit is actually contesting a battlefield right now, with Unmatched allowing that redirection to happen at instant speed against the opponent's own plays, and each reattachment onto Unrelenting refunding a card to keep the loop going. The win condition is winning Showdowns outright with a persistently well-equipped unit, sustained turn over turn by the draw-on-attach rather than by one big payoff — attrition through equipment efficiency, not a burst.

### Jayce, Defender of Tomorrow (Mind+Body)

Legend ability: `Empower for 2 Energy and two rainbow runes (use only if not Empowered). 1 Energy, Exhaust: Ready a gear. Empowered: 1 Energy, Exhaust: Ready 2 gear.` Champions: **Jayce, Brilliant Inventor** (Mind) — `When you play me or the first time you play a non-token gear each turn, you may ready something besides me that's exhausted.` **Jayce, Hammer in Hand** (Body) — `When I become ready, choose one to give me this turn — Assault 2 / Deflect 2 / Ganking.`

The Legend ability is a paid, repeatable readying engine for gear, upgradeable via Empower into readying two gear per activation instead of one — no opponent-dependent condition, just resources spent to generate extra "ready" events. Brilliant Inventor extends this beyond gear: playing a gear (the first each turn) or playing Jayce himself lets you ready something else that's exhausted, broadening the readying pool. Hammer in Hand is where this converges into a combo shape — every time Jayce becomes ready, by any means, he gets to choose a fresh combat keyword for the turn: Assault 2 to hit harder, Deflect 2 to survive interaction, or Ganking to reposition to another battlefield. That means each readying event isn't just "he can act again," it's a reconfigurable power-up each time.

Structurally, this rewards stacking multiple readying triggers into one turn — Empowered gear-readying plus Brilliant Inventor's own-turn readying — so Jayce can re-enter combat, defend safely, or Gank to a new location more than once before the turn ends, a compounding single-turn swing rather than a slow grind or a wide board. Without a Sound is comparatively grindier: conquering mints another Clone into your base (compounding attacker count turn over turn) and the swap ability repositions Zed and a Clone between locations — useful either to pull Zed to safety or to slot a fresh attacker into an active fight. Both Champions still require conquering or attacking to generate value, so neither pulls toward control; the split is between one explosive burst-combat build (From the Shadows) and one sustained multi-turn pressure build (Without a Sound) — a hybrid aggro/combo texture rather than a clean single lane.

Core unfolding: with From the Shadows, the early game discards cards to play Zed and stock the trash, a Shadow Clone token appears immediately as a disposable body; once it attacks, banishing a trash unit turns it into a one-shot Assault 4 threat capable of blowing through most blockers, and that same banish empowers the Legend for a loot activation to refill the hand for the next cycle. With Without a Sound, each successful conquer leaves behind another 0-might Clone, so board presence compounds the more you win, and the swap ability lets you keep threatening a fresh location without fully committing Zed himself to risk. Either way the engine converts into points by winning Showdowns outright — the Legend's own loot ability just sustains the hand so the aggression doesn't run out of gas.
