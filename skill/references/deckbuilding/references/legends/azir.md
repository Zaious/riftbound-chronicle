### Azir - Emperor of the Sands (Calm+Order)

> Tier 2-confirmed NOT CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — not a real split. Across tracked competitive Azir decks, Sovereign is a CORE include at ~77% presence; Ascendant appears in roughly 1% of Azir decks, and even that sliver is mostly her being splashed as a flex piece into *other* Legends' decks entirely, not an alternate Azir archetype. Real coverage describes the actual (Sovereign) deck as "control and value," a patient equipment-and-tokens shell — not the catalog's framed combo-vs-control fork.

Legend ability: `Your Sand Soldiers have Weaponmaster. 1 Energy, Exhaust: Play a 2 Might Sand Soldier unit token to your base. Use only if you've played an Equipment this turn.`
Azir - Ascendant (Calm): `A Calm rune: Action — Choose a unit you control. Move me to its location and it to my original location. If it's equipped, you may attach one of its Equipment to me. Use only once per turn.`
Azir - Sovereign (Order): `Accelerate (...) When I attack, you may move any number of your token units to this battlefield.`

**Archetype:** On paper the two Champions read as pulling in genuinely different directions, but real play doesn't support treating this as a fork — Sovereign is close to the only Champion actually played. The Legend ability is a repeatable, condition-gated engine — every turn you play an Equipment, you can spend energy to add another 2-might Sand Soldier token — which builds a wide board gradually over many turns, not in one burst. Sovereign then supplies a combo-shaped payoff: `When I attack, you may move any number of your token units to this battlefield` collapses a slowly-accumulated, dispersed token army onto a single Showdown, converting patient setup into one decisive swing. Ascendant, by contrast, is a positional utility tool — swap places with a unit and optionally steal its Equipment — that supports steadier, incremental equipment value and protecting key pieces rather than any single climactic turn; it reads as control/grind-adjacent, with no scoring text of its own.

**Core unfolding:** Early game requires playing an Equipment every turn just to keep the token engine online at all — that's a hard gate, not a suggestion, since the token-play ability explicitly does nothing without an Equipment played that same turn. With Sovereign, the deck spends several turns quietly building a wide, individually unthreatening board of 2-might tokens across multiple battlefields, then uses Sovereign's attack trigger to mass-mobilize all of them onto one contested battlefield at once, turning an otherwise unremarkable token spread into an overwhelming single Showdown. With Ascendant instead, the same token/Equipment base is used more defensively — repositioning to dodge removal aimed at a key equipped unit and consolidating Equipment onto Azir himself.

**Win condition:** with Sovereign — essentially the only Champion this Legend is actually played with — many turns of quiet token accumulation cash in on one attack to win a Showdown outright through sudden numbers, a real combo line. Ascendant's text is coherent as a defensive utility tool, but real deckbuilders don't build a separate deck around it; treat the "Ascendant control build" as an unbuilt Tier 1 hypothesis, not a real alternative.
