### Poppy - Keeper of the Hammer (Body+Order)

Legend ability: `When you hold, gain 1 XP. Spend 3 XP, Exhaust: Draw 1.` **Poppy - Paragon** (Body): `Deflect. When you play me, if an opponent's score is within 3 points of the Victory Score, ready me and gain 3 XP.` **Poppy - Defender of the Meek** (Order): `You may spend 3 XP as an additional cost to play me. If you do, I cost 3 Energy less. Ambush. Tank.`

"When you hold, gain 1 XP" is a compounding, ongoing trigger in the same family as end-of-turn effects — it pays out repeatedly for as long as a Battlefield stays held, not once. It converts that patience directly into card draw (spend 3 XP to draw), meaning the entire Legend kit is holding Battlefields for its own sake, then turning sustained control of the board into resources. Both Champions reinforce a defensive read rather than complicating it. Paragon is an emergency stabilizer: its bonus (ready itself, gain 3 XP immediately) only triggers when the opponent is dangerously close to winning (within 3 of Victory Score) — a panic-button built for exactly the moment a control deck needs to buy one more turn. Defender of the Meek spends the same XP pool as a discount and gains Ambush (can be dropped reactively onto a Battlefield you already hold) plus Tank (forced to absorb combat damage first) — a reinforcement that arrives on the opponent's move rather than needing to be pre-committed, and that's built to die absorbing damage so other units survive. Nothing in this kit wants an early race or a stored-up alpha turn; it wants to hold cheaply, draw off the XP that generates, and keep replacing/reinforcing defenders as the game stretches on. This is control with no real ambiguity. Points: the XP engine is the win condition's own resource loop made literal — hold a Battlefield, gain XP, spend XP to draw into the next answer or the next cheap Defender, and use Paragon specifically as insurance against losing the long grind to a fast opponent.
