### Rengar - Pridestalker (Fury+Body)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — the proactive/aggro identity and Trophy Hunter's Ambush-into-a-contested-battlefield role are real (hextechanalytics.com names Trophy Hunter "the common chosen Champion," and a 2026-08-08 Riftbound Showdown Ottawa 1st-place list ran him CHOSEN with zero copies of Unseen). But Unseen is not a comparable partner in practice — riftDecks.com has it FRINGE at just 4.55% presence with a below-average 0.74x top-cut rate, essentially unplayed and underperforming when it is included, unlike the roughly-equal weighting this entry originally gave both Champions.

> Follow-up 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — a source-data pipeline bug (fixed in `extract_legend_packets.py`) meant this entry never considered Rengar, Pouncing (Fury, SFD-025/221), a real third Champion print. It doesn't change this entry's conclusion: within Rengar's own decks it's FLEX at just 9.09% presence with a below-average 0.68x top-cut rate (riftDecks.com, accessed 2026-08-18) — worse-performing than the already-fringe Unseen, despite roughly double its presence — and it's played in far more decks overall (289+ in the last month) as a generic Reaction/Assault 2 splash in other Legends (Annie, Dark Child foremost) than as a Rengar card specifically. hextechanalytics.com's current Rengar guide doesn't mention it at all. Trophy Hunter remains the only real Champion choice for this Legend.

> Errata-corrected 2026-08-17 (`references/errata_overlay.json`, Unleashed Errata Updates, 2026-04-03) — Trophy Hunter's ability text below reflects the official wording fix (ties the effect explicitly to the Ambush keyword rather than describing it as a separate "can be played" permission). No mechanical change from the pre-errata text; the analysis is unaffected.

Legend ability: `When you play a unit, give a unit +1 might this turn.` **Rengar - Unseen** (Fury): `Accelerate. Assault 2. Deflect. Ganking.` **Rengar - Trophy Hunter** (Body): `Ambush. I can Ambush to a battlefield where there are enemy units, even if you don't have units there.`

Archetype: aggro — a proactive, unit-density deck that wants to seize an early lead and pilot the game from ahead, close to how real coverage frames it ("builds to play a very proactive game plan, designed to quickly seize the lead," riftbound.gg). The Legend triggers off simply playing a unit — immediate, unconditional, and repeatable every time a body hits the board — which rewards a wide, cheap, frequently-refilled curve rather than any single big payoff. **In real play, Rengar - Trophy Hunter is this Legend's actual default Champion** (CORE, 81.17% presence, 1.08x top-cut rate) — Ambush lets it drop straight into a battlefield the opponent already occupies, forcing a fight on arrival instead of waiting to build up local presence first. **Rengar - Unseen, despite its text reading as an equally aggressive toolbox (Accelerate, Assault 2, Deflect, Ganking), is essentially unplayed in real decks** (FRINGE, 4.55% presence, 0.74x — below-average even in the few lists that run it) and does not appear in real tournament-winning lists at all.

Core unfolding: early game wants a steady stream of cheap unit plays — each one immediately triggers the Legend's +1 might buff, so raw frequency of plays generates value with no combo piece required. Real winning lists sequence several unit plays right before the Showdown that matters, spreading Rengar's temporary Might and cards like Pit Rookie's permanent buff across whichever battlefields need it (hextechanalytics.com, "Rengar's Field Guide"). Trophy Hunter is the piece that closes out fights: it can be dropped directly into a contested battlefield to swing a fight immediately rather than needing to first establish a beachhead there. Closing out is a matter of accumulated small might buffs plus a proactive board overwhelming battlefields the opponent hasn't stabilized yet — not, as the text-only reading suggested, Unseen's keyword suite, which real decks mostly don't run.

Win condition: winning Showdowns outright through sheer combat frequency and stat advantage — this Legend has no holding-oriented or resource-generation angle in its text at all; everything converts directly into combat pressure.
