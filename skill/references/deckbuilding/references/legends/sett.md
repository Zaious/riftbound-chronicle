### Sett - The Boss (Body+Order)

Legend ability: `When a buffed unit you control would die, you may pay a rainbow rune and exhaust me to spend its buff and recall it exhausted instead. When you conquer, ready me.` **Sett, Brawler** (Body): `When you play me or when I conquer, buff me. Spend my buff: Give me +4 might this turn.` **Sett - Kingpin** (Order): `Tank. I get +1 might for each buffed friendly unit at my battlefield.`

Archetype: control/midrange, with a combo-adjacent option inside Brawler. The Legend's core mechanic is a reactive death-insurance clause — it doesn't fire on a schedule, it fires conditionally whenever a buffed unit would otherwise be lost, converting a bad combat trade into merely spending a buff and a rune to recall the unit instead. That's a resilience mechanic, not a race mechanic: it exists specifically so the deck can keep re-fighting with the same accumulated investment rather than bleeding board state. The conquer clause (ready Sett) reinforces that this insurance stays available across repeated engagements rather than being a one-time save.

Brawler buffs itself on play or conquer and can later cash that buff in for a one-turn +4 might spike — a bank-now, detonate-later pattern that swings a single Showdown decisively, the deck's one genuinely combo-flavored piece. Kingpin instead scales passively off the number of buffed friendly units already sitting at its battlefield, rewarding clustering multiple buffed bodies together rather than banking one big spender, and Tank means it (not its buffed companions) absorbs damage first.

Core unfolding: early game is about getting units buffed (via Brawler's play/conquer trigger) rather than winning fights outright. Mid-game, the Legend's recall-instead-of-die clause is what lets the deck keep committing those buffed units to combat repeatedly without attrition costing it the board — this is the actual engine, a resilience loop fueled by conquering. Closing out, Brawler spends its banked buff for a sudden four-might swing to force a Showdown win, while Kingpin's payoff is a slow-built, hard-to-crack stack of buffed defenders at one battlefield.

Win condition: primarily holding via resilience — buffed threats that would die get recalled instead, keeping a defended battlefield defended turn after turn — with Brawler layering in an outright-win-the-Showdown burst option on top of that base plan.
