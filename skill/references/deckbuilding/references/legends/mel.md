### Mel - Soul's Reflection (Mind+Chaos)

Legend ability: `When you empower something else, empower me. (I become Empowered if I'm not already.) Disempower me, Exhaust: Give a unit at a battlefield -2 Might this turn.` **Mel, Newly Awakened** (Mind): `When you play me, draw 1. Empower 3 Energy. Empowered: Your spells and abilities can't be countered. If a spell or ability you control would give -Might to a unit it chooses, it gives an additional -1 Might.` **Mel, Defiant Soul** (Chaos): `Empower — Discard a spell. When I become Empowered, banish an enemy unit at a battlefield with 3 Might or less.`

The Legend's own trigger is reactive and derivative — it doesn't fire on its own timing, it piggybacks on Empower events happening elsewhere in the deck ("when you empower something else"), meaning Mel's payoff scales with how many Empower-capable pieces the deck runs, not with any single big turn. The payoff itself, disempowering Mel to shave -2 might off an enemy unit, is a repeatable removal-adjacent tool, not a burst finisher. Both Champions confirm a denial-engine reading rather than a race: Newly Awakened's Empowered state protects the deck's spells/abilities from being countered and adds an extra -1 might onto any -might effect already being applied — amplifying the shrink-based removal rather than replacing it with damage or destroy effects. Defiant Soul pays for its own Empower by discarding a spell (a real resource cost, not free) and its payoff for becoming Empowered is an outright banish of a small enemy unit (3 might or less) — conditional, cheap-target removal rather than an unconditional sweep. None of this compounds into a single explosive turn; it's a steady, multi-piece engine that needs several Empower triggers across a game to keep firing, which is a control texture (patient denial over time) with a combo-adjacent setup requirement (needs synergy pieces already assembled to function at all) rather than a genuine combo detonation. Points: this deck doesn't push its own units forward to conquer so much as it shrinks and picks off the opponent's small units so that Showdowns the opponent should win are lost instead, letting friendly Battlefields go uncontested or letting cheap units hold ground that would otherwise be overrun.
