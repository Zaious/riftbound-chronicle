### Jhin - Virtuoso (Fury+Mind)

Legend ability: `When you play a spell, if you spent 4+ Energy, you may banish it. Then, if there are four spells banished with me, put each in its trash, channel 4 runes, and draw 1.` Champions: **Jhin - Murderous Artist** (Fury) — `Deflect. Ganking. When I move, add 1 Energy and a rainbow rune.` **Jhin - Meticulous Killer** (Mind) — `Vision. If you've spent 4+ Energy to play a spell this turn, you may play me for a Mind rune.`

The Legend ability is a slow-burn counting combo: every time you cast a spell costing 4+ Energy, you may bank it (banished, not trashed), and only once four such spells have accumulated does it detonate — trashing all four, channeling 4 runes, and drawing a card. That's about as explicit a "patient setup, then one big payoff" trigger as exists in this pool: it requires four separate expensive-spell castings across a game before it fires once. Meticulous Killer feeds this plan directly — if you've already spent 4+ Energy on a spell that turn, he comes down for just a Mind rune, letting the same big-spell turn also deploy your Champion for free.

Murderous Artist, however, does not feed this at all. Deflect and Ganking with move-triggered resource generation is a mobile, tempo-forward kit built around repositioning across battlefields, unrelated to spells or the 4-cost threshold — choosing him effectively abandons the Legend's own payoff condition in favor of a faster, more direct plan. This Legend genuinely splits by Champion choice rather than committing to one lane: with Meticulous Killer, the deck plays combo — grind through the early-to-mid game casting expensive spells (banking toward four), pay for Meticulous Killer's discount off that same spending, and eventually detonate into a resource burst (4 runes, a card) that indirectly funds whatever finishing plays follow, since the payoff itself scores nothing. With Murderous Artist instead, the deck ignores the banish-and-detonate plan almost entirely and instead plays a faster, mobility-funded game — moving between battlefields for Energy and runes to directly contest and win Showdowns turn over turn, closer to aggressive tempo than to the Legend's own combo core. Which Champion is chosen meaningfully changes what kind of deck this is, and that split is the honest finding here rather than a single clean archetype call.
