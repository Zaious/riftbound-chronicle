### Volibear, Relentless Storm (Fury+Body)

> Tier 2-confirmed NOT CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — this is not a real Fury-aggro/Body-control fork. Real coverage treats Volibear as one unified ramp deck, and is explicit that neither Champion is actually the deck's payoff: Furious sits in the chosen-Champion slot mainly because he's cheaper than Imposing, "but rarely is played unless we have little else to do for the turn." Imposing is barely a Volibear card in practice at all — tournament data shows it's overwhelmingly played as a generic big-body splash in *other* Legends' decks (Jayce, Jax, Lee Sin, Master Yi, Poppy), not as an alternative Volibear archetype. The real deck's actual payoffs are non-Champion cards (Deadbloom Predator, Stormbringer, Dazzling Aurora, Kadregrin the Infernal), and its own coverage rates it as niche: "definitely not one of the best lists overall."

Legend ability: `When you play a Mighty unit, you may exhaust me to channel 1 rune exhausted. (Mighty = 5+ might)` **Volibear, Furious** (Fury): `Deflect 2. When I attack, deal 5 damage split among any number of enemy units here.` **Volibear, Imposing** (Body): `Shield 3. Tank. When an opponent moves to a battlefield other than mine, draw 1.`

The Legend ability itself is archetype-neutral ramp: it only cares that you played a 5+ might unit, converting that into an extra resource. On paper, Furious (attack-triggered AoE, an aggressive removal tool) and Imposing (a passive Shield/Tank wall that draws off the opponent's movement) read as pulling toward opposite plans — but real practice doesn't build around either one as a defining payoff. This is a **single ramp/midrange shell**, not a fork: the deck's actual identity comes from what the Mighty-unit ramp funds elsewhere in the list (Deadbloom Predator, Stormbringer, Dazzling Aurora, Kadregrin the Infernal), with Furious along for the ride in the Champion slot mostly because he costs less than Imposing, not because his AoE is the plan. Imposing specifically doesn't support a real "Body-control Volibear" — real tournament data shows it played almost exclusively as a big stat-stick splashed into unrelated Body-domain Legend decks (Jayce, Jax, Lee Sin, Master Yi, Poppy), not chosen as this Legend's own Champion.

Core unfolding: because the ramp trigger needs a Mighty (5+) unit already in play, the early turns are about hitting that threshold as soon as the curve allows — a deck too light on big bodies never gets to use the Legend ability at all. From there, the deck plays as a ramp/midrange shell: channel extra runes off big-unit drops, deploy the list's real top-end (not the Champion) as those runes come online, and use Furious passively as a cheap, present-but-secondary body in the Champion slot rather than an active AoE-removal plan. This is a real but narrow archetype — competitive coverage frames it as strong in specific niche formats (4x4) rather than a top-tier Constructed line.

**Win condition:** winning Showdowns through the ramped-into top-end threats, not through either Champion's own printed ability — Furious's AoE and Imposing's draw engine are both secondary to the deck's actual plan, which is reaching its expensive non-Champion payoffs faster than a curve-out opponent can answer them.
