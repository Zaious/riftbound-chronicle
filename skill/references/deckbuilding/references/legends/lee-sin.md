### Lee Sin - Blind Monk (Calm+Body)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — the Control/midrange, buff-stacking-toward-a-hold identity is solidly real (riftboundguide.com describes it as "slow, buff-based, defensive," about "building a position, protecting your strongest unit"). But the "two Champions splitting into different shapes of the same lane" framing overstates parity: Lee Sin, Ascetic was effectively cut from competitive play once Spiritforged shipped, "completely left out... as the card typically takes too long to grow for the rewards it carries," and real play-rate data shows Centered far ahead (~48 decks/month vs. Ascetic's ~8). Centered is the real sole build, and the Legend overall is weak (Tier 4, "the weakest of the three Origins Champion Decks").

Legend ability: `1 Energy, Exhaust: Buff a friendly unit. (If it doesn't have a buff, it gets a +1 might buff.)` **Lee Sin - Ascetic** (Calm): `Shield. Exhaust: Buff me. (+1 might buff.) I can have any number of buffs.` **Lee Sin - Centered** (Body): `Accelerate. Other buffed friendly units at my battlefield have +2 might.` (Real decks run Centered almost exclusively — see the callout above; Ascetic's stacking plan is a real textual reading but was pushed out of competitive play once the format moved past Origins.)

**Archetype**: Control/midrange holds up well against real coverage, though "with the two Champions splitting into different shapes of the same lane" doesn't match current practice. The Legend ability is a cheap, player-initiated, repeatable buff generator with no combat or turn-phase gate — it rewards sustained, turn-over-turn investment rather than a burst or a race, and that part is confirmed by real sources describing the deck as slow and defensive. Ascetic's textual case (exploiting that most units cap at one buff instance, turning repeated activations into an unbounded single threat) was real in the earliest Origins-format meta but is not a currently competitive lane. Centered is the real default: her static bonus (+2 Might to any buffed unit at her battlefield) rewards width, and this is the version featured in current guides and the prebuilt Champion Deck.

**Core unfolding**: Early game wants a unit at a contested battlefield receiving cheap, repeated buffs from the Legend ability — confirmed by real coverage. In real practice this plays out through Centered: the deck spreads buffs across multiple units to convert Centered's flat +2 into a group-wide advantage, rather than concentrating investment onto a single Ascetic body (a plan real coverage says stopped being worth playing once the format sped up). The payoff is stat superiority accrued gradually, and real sources confirm the closing shape directly: "building a position, protecting your strongest unit, and making it very hard for your opponent to take battlefields away from you."

**Engine to points**: Control/attrition is confirmed — Might inflation converts into winning, then holding, Showdowns turn after turn, matching real coverage's "slow, buff-based, defensive" framing closely. Nothing here generates cards or independent resources; the plan is stat accumulation in service of ground control. Worth flagging alongside the mechanical confirmation: this Legend is currently the weakest of the three Origins Champion Decks competitively (Tier 4), a viability caveat the text-only derivation has no way to surface.
