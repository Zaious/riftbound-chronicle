### Lillia - Bashful Bloom (Calm+Mind)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — real coverage runs this as an aggro/pressure-conquest deck (Cardsrealm: "Lillia Aggro," "the fastest strategy in the game... can win through pressure alone," scoring "2 points per turn nearly all the time") rather than the "combo-leaning" label used here — no real source calls it combo. Lillia, Protector of Dreams is essentially unplayed in practice (riftDecks.com: FRINGE, 0.75% presence) while Lillia, Fae Fawn is confirmed the real default Champion (CORE, 75.94% presence; named "Main Champion" by Cardsrealm) rather than a comparably-weighted partner.

Legend ability: `4 Energy, Exhaust: Play a ready 3 might Sprite unit token with Temporary. This ability costs 1 Energy less for each friendly unit with Temporary.` **Lillia - Protector of Dreams** (Calm): `When you play a token unit, give me +1 might this turn. Your token units have Tank.` **Lillia - Fae Fawn** (Mind): `Accelerate. When I move from a location, play a 3 might Sprite unit token with Temporary there.`

**Archetype**: Text-derivation called this "combo-leaning swarm," reasoning that a snowballing, self-discounting token engine built on single-window disposable bodies is neither classic control nor steady aggro. Real coverage disagrees with that label from multiple directions rather than converging on "combo": Cardsrealm's guide is titled "Lillia Aggro" and calls this "the fastest strategy in the game," built to "score 2 points per turn nearly all the time" through constant per-turn Showdown pressure; riftbound.gg's own guide calls it a "value oriented play style" instead; hextechanalytics calls it "control-focused tempo." No real source uses "combo." The underlying mechanic still holds up well: the Legend ability is a self-discounting token generator, each Temporary unit already on board making the next one cheaper, and Cardsrealm confirms the same sequencing logic ("always counting how many temporary units you can put in play each turn so that you can use her effect for less energies"). Because these tokens are Temporary (they die before their controller's next Beginning Phase, before scoring), they're explicitly disposable, single-window pieces — real coverage frames their job as trading against the opponent's real units so a real unit can conquer instead, or tying up blockers to force free conquers elsewhere, which is fast turn-by-turn pressure rather than a patient held-back detonation. Fae Fawn is the real, confirmed default Champion (see callout above) and does add a second, movement-triggered token source plus Accelerate. Protector of Dreams, despite reading on paper as a comparably reinforcing partner (Tank-granting, growing with every token played), is essentially unplayed in practice — not a genuine second half of this Legend's identity.

**Core unfolding**: Early turns want the first Temporary token or two out — via full-cost Legend activation or Fae Fawn's move-trigger — purely to start discounting the ability. Once several are in play, the same Energy that bought one token now buys another, so a single turn can generate a disproportionate number of 3-Might Tank bodies at a contested battlefield right before a Showdown. Because the tokens expire before scoring, the plan is built around winning that Showdown this turn or next with sheer body count, not settling in afterward.

**Engine to points**: Resource investment converts directly into winning a specific Showdown outright through body count and Tank-protected combat math — a burst-of-bodies plan aimed at conquering, not at holding afterward (the tokens are gone by the next scoring step) and not at generating cards or resources.
