### Kha'Zix - Voidreaver (Body+Chaos)

> Tier 2-confirmed NOT CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — real coverage (hextechanalytics.com, riftbound.gg) names Kha'Zix, Mutating Horror the "Chosen Champion" and labels the deck "Tempo" / "Tempo-midrange," not the Control/attrition read below. Play-rate data confirms it isn't a comparably-weighted split either: Mutating Horror has been played in 1,319+ decks in the last month per riftDecks.com against roughly 6 for Evolving Hunter, who is essentially unplayed rather than an "aggressive branch of the same grind engine."

Legend ability: `When you win a combat, gain 1 XP. Spend 1 XP, Exhaust: Buff a unit. Spend 2 XP, Exhaust: Move an exhausted friendly unit from a battlefield to its base.` **Kha'Zix - Evolving Hunter** (Body): `Hunt. When I conquer or hold, gain 1 XP. When I attack, you may spend 3 XP to deal damage equal to my Might to an enemy unit here.` **Kha'Zix - Mutating Horror** (Chaos): `Ambush. When I attack or defend, if an enemy unit is alone here, give me +2 might this turn and gain 2 XP.` (Real decks run Mutating Horror almost exclusively — see the callout above; Evolving Hunter is not a genuine second lane in practice.)

**Archetype**: Text alone reads as Control/attrition — the Legend's trigger (win a combat, gain XP) is reactive to combat resolution and feeds a resource pool spent on utility (buff a unit, or pull an exhausted unit to safety), which looks like reinvestment into keeping the same units strong and alive across many turns rather than racing. But real competitive coverage doesn't build it that way: hextechanalytics.com calls the real archetype "Tempo," and riftbound.gg describes a "Tempo-midrange strategy focused on XP gain & conquest." The real engine actively manufactures favorable fights rather than grinding out existing ones — Void Assault "reshapes both sides of a battlefield before combat begins" specifically to set up the isolated 1v1s that trigger Mutating Horror's Ambush bonus, and XP funds the next favorable engagement rather than sitting as a defensive attrition resource.

**Core unfolding**: Early game wants winnable fights, but in real practice this means actively engineering isolated 1v1s for Mutating Horror (via movement and reshaping effects like Void Assault) rather than just taking favorable Showdowns as they come. Once running, XP funds ongoing stat buffs and repositioning that keep the tempo plan moving; the 2-XP recall-to-base spend protects a threatened unit from a bad trade so it can fight again, but the overall shape real decks describe is proactive combat-selection, not a slow attrition grind.

**Engine to points**: The Legend-level mechanism (win combat → XP → buff/reposition, Hunt rewarding holding) is read correctly at the text level and does convert combat success into board control. What the text-only derivation gets wrong is the pacing: real decks play this as a tempo/midrange plan built around Mutating Horror's isolation triggers (with key support like Fizz the Trickster, Lucian the Merciless, and Rengar, Trophy Hunter), not a patient win-then-hold control shell anchored on Evolving Hunter, who real players essentially don't run.
