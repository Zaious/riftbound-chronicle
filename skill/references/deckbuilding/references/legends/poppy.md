### Poppy - Keeper of the Hammer (Body+Order)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — hextechanalytics.com confirms the hold-into-XP-into-draw engine almost verbatim ("generating XP from each successful Hold... spending three XP to exhaust Poppy for a card draw") and confirms Poppy, Paragon as "the standard chosen Champion" (93.3% real inclusion per riftwatcher.com, vs. Defender of the Meek at roughly 11 decks/month per riftDecks.com — a rare tech pick, not a comparably-weighted reinforcing partner). But real coverage labels the archetype **Midrange**, not this entry's "Control" — riftwatcher.com rates the Legend Tier A with a 52.5% win rate.

Legend ability: `When you hold, gain 1 XP. Spend 3 XP, Exhaust: Draw 1.` **Poppy - Paragon** (Body): `Deflect. When you play me, if an opponent's score is within 3 points of the Victory Score, ready me and gain 3 XP.` **Poppy - Defender of the Meek** (Order): `You may spend 3 XP as an additional cost to play me. If you do, I cost 3 Energy less. Ambush. Tank.` (Real decks build overwhelmingly around Paragon — see the callout above; Defender of the Meek is a real but rare inclusion, not an equally-weighted partner.)

"When you hold, gain 1 XP" is a compounding, ongoing trigger in the same family as end-of-turn effects — it pays out repeatedly for as long as a Battlefield stays held, not once. It converts that patience directly into card draw (spend 3 XP to draw), meaning the entire Legend kit is holding Battlefields for its own sake, then turning sustained control of the board into resources — a read real coverage backs up closely, even if it prefers the word "Midrange" to "Control." Paragon is an emergency stabilizer: its bonus (ready itself, gain 3 XP immediately) only triggers when the opponent is dangerously close to winning (within 3 of Victory Score) — a panic-button built for exactly the moment this deck needs to buy one more turn, and real play confirms Paragon is the deck's actual default rather than one of two comparable options. Defender of the Meek spends the same XP pool as a discount and gains Ambush (can be dropped reactively onto a Battlefield you already hold) plus Tank (forced to absorb combat damage first) — a real, legal reinforcement piece, but current competitive lists mostly don't run her; treat this as a text-plausible tech option rather than a load-bearing partner to Paragon. Nothing in this kit wants an early race or a stored-up alpha turn; it wants to hold cheaply, draw off the XP that generates, and keep replacing/reinforcing defenders as the game stretches on — real coverage frames this as a patient midrange plan built around defending one battlefield and banking XP for the draw that matters, rather than the book's own "control" label. Points: the XP engine is the win condition's own resource loop made literal — hold a Battlefield, gain XP, spend XP to draw into the next answer, and use Paragon specifically as insurance against losing the long grind to a fast opponent.
