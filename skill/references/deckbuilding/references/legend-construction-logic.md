# Legend construction logic (catalog)

This file exists because the method demonstrated on Kai'Sa and Annie in the main deckbuilding book scales cleanly to the full roster — the derivation is grounded in permanent card text, not a community-guide snapshot, so it doesn't carry the staleness risk that would come from writing up 46 entries' worth of "what people currently play." What *would* go stale (a specific named supporting card, a specific tournament decklist) simply isn't in this file — only the structural reading of each Legend's own printed kit is. Read the main book's methodology section first; this is the reference table it produces when run across the whole current roster, not a substitute for understanding the method itself.

### Ahri - Nine-Tailed Fox (Calm+Mind)

Legend ability: `When an enemy unit attacks a battlefield you control, give it -1 Might this turn, to a minimum of 1 Might.` This is a reactive, defensive trigger — it only fires when the opponent attacks a battlefield Ahri already controls, structurally rewarding a holding/control posture rather than proactive pushes. **Ahri, Inquisitive** (Mind): `When I attack or defend, give an enemy unit here -2 Might this turn, to a minimum of 1 Might.` — a stronger version of the same debuff (-2 vs. -1), usable on either side of combat. **Ahri - Alluring** (Calm): `When I hold, you score 1 point.` — direct point generation simply for holding a battlefield, no combat required. The three pieces reinforce each other tightly: all three revolve around holding battlefields and weakening attackers rather than killing them outright, with Alluring converting that holding state straight into points. Cross-referencing the Domain table: Mind's card pool leads all Domains in opponent-stat-weakening effects (12%, next-highest only 2%) — Inquisitive's -2 Might debuff is a direct hit of exactly that signature mechanic. Calm's pool has the lowest direct-damage share of any Domain (2%) and "not built for proactive aggression" as its stated weakness — Ahri's kit never deals damage at all, consistent with that; Alluring's passive point-scoring from holding also fits Calm's non-aggressive, "peace of holding" design intent almost literally. Construction logic: a Legend built around softening attackers with Might debuffs while camped on held battlefields, scoring through passive holding rather than combat kills.

### Akali - Rogue Assassin (Fury+Calm)

Legend ability: `Empower 3 Energy a rainbow rune (Empower this. Use only if not Empowered.) Action: Exhaust: If it's your turn, move a friendly unit in a showdown to base and if I'm Empowered, ready it.` This is a conditional extraction tool: pull a unit out of an active fight back to base, and once Empowered, the extraction also readies that unit. **Akali, Deadly Weapon** (Fury): `Empower 2 Energy a Fury rune When I move, you may deal 1 to a unit at a battlefield I moved to or from. If I'm Empowered, deal 2 instead. Empowered: I have +1 Might.` — converts movement into direct damage. **Akali, Silent** (Calm): `I can't be chosen by enemy spells and abilities unless I'm in combat. When I move to a battlefield, give me +2 Might this turn.` — converts movement into protection and a temporary buff, never damage. Both Champions key off the same trigger as the Legend (moving a unit), so mechanically they reinforce it, but they diverge sharply in purpose: Deadly Weapon turns movement into offense, Silent turns it into survivability. Notably, Fury+Calm is one of the three official opposite-Domain axes, historically the rarest pairing type (4 dual-Domain cards vs. 11-18 for non-opposite pairs) — worth flagging as thinner-supported by default. Cross-referencing the table, the split lands cleanly along each Domain's known strength: Fury's pool leads in direct damage (17%, highest of six), matching Deadly Weapon's move-triggered damage; Calm's pool has the lowest direct-damage share (2%) and its stated weakness is exactly "not built for proactive aggression" — Silent, appropriately, never deals damage and instead protects/buffs. Construction logic: a Legend built around repeatedly moving units out of combat, with the Champion choice deciding whether that movement is empowered into damage output or into defensive protection.

### Ambessa - Matriarch of War (Body+Order)

Legend ability: `When you empower something else, empower me. (I become Empowered if I'm not already.) Disempower me, a rainbow rune, Exhaust: Ready a unit.` This is a two-stage engine: Ambessa becomes Empowered as a free side effect of empowering anything else, then that stored Empowered state can be spent (disempower + rune + exhaust) to ready a unit. **Ambessa, The Wolf** (Body): `Empower 3 Energy a Body rune Empowered: I have +3 Might and can't be dealt damage unless I'm in combat.` — a durable, stat-heavy payoff for being Empowered. **Ambessa, Respected and Feared** (Order): `Empower 1 Energy a Order rune a Order rune Empowered: I have [Assault 2]. Empowered: When I attack, kill an enemy unit here with less Might than me.` — an aggressive, destroy-on-attack payoff for being Empowered. Both Champions reinforce the Legend's central resource (Empower) but cash it out into opposite gameplans once Empowered: The Wolf becomes a near-unkillable stat stick, Respected and Feared becomes a removal-on-attack threat. Cross-referencing the table, both align tightly with their Domain's known identity: Order's pool leads all Domains in destroy-removal by a wide margin (32% vs. next-highest 15%), and Respected and Feared's "kill an enemy unit here with less Might than me" is exactly that mechanic; Body's pool leads in 6+ Might units (27%) and average Might (4.5), both matched directly by The Wolf's flat +3 Might bonus. Construction logic: a Legend built around generating Empowered status as a byproduct of empowering other cards, then spending that stored resource to either tank (Body) or kill on attack (Order), depending on the Champion chosen.

### Azir - Emperor of the Sands (Calm+Order)

Legend ability: `Your Sand Soldiers have [Weaponmaster]. 1 Energy, Exhaust: Play a 2 Might Sand Soldier unit token to your base. Use only if you've played an Equipment this turn.` This gates token generation behind having already played an Equipment that turn, and grants the resulting tokens an Equipment-related keyword — an Equipment-into-tokens engine. **Azir - Ascendant** (Calm): `a Calm rune: Action — Choose a unit you control. Move me to its location and it to my original location. If it's equipped, you may attach one of its Equipment to me. Use only once per turn.` — a once-per-turn swap that can transfer Equipment onto Azir. **Azir - Sovereign** (Order): `[Accelerate] (You may pay 1 Energy a Order rune as an additional cost to have me enter ready.) When I attack, you may move any number of your token units to this battlefield.` — enters ready for a price, and rallies token units into the attack, directly consuming the Legend's Sand Soldier output. The two Champions split the Legend ability's two ingredients rather than converging on one: Sovereign builds directly on the token half, Ascendant builds on the Equipment half. Cross-referencing the table: Order's pool leads in destroy-removal (32%) and death-benefit (12%), but neither Champion shows a destroy or death-benefit angle — Sovereign's token rally is a board-presence tool, not removal, so Azir's Order half doesn't visibly connect to Order's known table identity here, worth stating plainly rather than forcing a link. Calm's pool has the lowest direct-damage share (2%) and a stated aggression weakness — Ascendant fits that consistently, dealing no damage and offering only repositioning/Equipment utility. Construction logic: a Legend built around chaining Equipment plays into recurring Sand Soldier tokens, with the Champion choice deciding whether that engine rallies tokens into the attack or spends its energy on repositioning and Equipment transfer.

### Darius - Hand of Noxus (Fury+Order)

Legend ability: `Exhaust: Reaction, Legion — Add 1 Energy. (Abilities that add resources can't be reacted to. Get the effect if you've played a card this turn.)` A Reaction-timed Energy generator gated on the Legion condition — needing a card already played that turn to fire. **Darius - Trifarian** (Fury): `When you play your second card in a turn, give me +2 Might this turn and ready me.` — rewards a second card played in a turn with a stat buff and a ready. **Darius - Executioner** (Order): `Legion — When you play me, ready me. (Get the effect if you've played another card this turn) Other friendly units have +1 Might here.` — also explicitly Legion-conditioned, plus a static local Might buff. All three pieces of text — Legend and both Champions — key off the identical "played a/another card already this turn" condition, making this an unusually tightly unified trio: a turn where you commit multiple cards pays off with extra Energy, extra Might and a ready, and a ready-on-entry plus a board buff, all at once. Cross-referencing the table: neither Champion's payoff visibly matches its Domain's table-leading mechanic — Fury's signature is direct damage (17%) but Trifarian's payoff is stat/readying, not damage; Order's signature is destroy-removal (32%) but Executioner's payoff is a buff and a ready, not removal. Worth noting plainly rather than forcing a connection — the Legion mechanic here reads as its own cross-Domain throughline independent of either Domain's typical card-pool profile. Construction logic: a Legend built around consistently playing multiple cards per turn to satisfy a shared Legion condition across the Legend and both Champions, converting that multi-card turn into extra Energy, Might, and readied units.

### Diana - Scorn of the Moon (Mind+Chaos)

Legend ability: `Reaction: Exhaust: Add 1 Energy. Spend this Energy only during showdowns. (Abilities that add resources can't be reacted to.)` The Energy this generates is restricted to combat (showdown) use only, signaling a plan built around combat-timed spending rather than general curve development. **Diana - Lunari** (Mind): `When a showdown begins here, you may pay 1 Energy. If you do, [Predict], then reveal the top card of your Main Deck. If it's a spell, draw it. (To Predict, look at the top card of your Main Deck. You may recycle it.)` — spends showdown-restricted Energy (matching the Legend directly) to dig for a spell when a fight starts. **Diana - No Longer Human** (Chaos): `[Ambush] (You may play me as a Reaction to a battlefield where you have units.) When you play a spell, give me +2 Might this turn.` — can be deployed reactively into an existing fight, and scales with spells played. Both Champions are explicitly showdown/combat-timed, reinforcing the Legend's combat-restricted Energy pool, and they form a feedback loop: Lunari finds spells during a fight, No Longer Human turns spells played into combat Might. Cross-referencing the table: Mind's pool leads all Domains in draw (17%) — Lunari's Predict-then-draw is exactly that specialty. Chaos's pool leads in movement/relocation (24%) and trash-recursion (20%), but No Longer Human's Ambush is a reactive-deployment keyword rather than the relocation mechanic the table measures, and it touches neither trash-recursion nor movement directly — this Champion doesn't visibly connect to either of Chaos's table-leading mechanics, worth saying plainly rather than stretching for a link. Construction logic: a Legend built around banking Energy that only unlocks during combat, spent to dig for spells and convert those spells into reactively-deployed combat strength.

### Draven - Glorious Executioner (Fury+Chaos)

Legend ability: `When you win a combat, draw 1. (You win if only your units remain after combat.)` An immediate, combat-outcome-triggered draw — card advantage tied directly to winning fights, with no setup condition beyond the win itself. **Draven - Vanquisher** (Fury): `When I win a combat, play a Gold gear token exhausted. When I attack or defend, you may pay a Fury rune. If you do, give me +2 Might this turn.` — stacks a gear-token payoff on the same win-a-combat trigger, plus a paid Might pump usable in any combat. **Draven - Audacious** (Chaos): `[Deflect] (Opponents must pay a rainbow rune to choose me with a spell or ability.) The first time I win a combat each turn, you score 1 point. When I die in combat, choose an opponent. They score 1 point.` — converts the same win-a-combat trigger straight into points instead of cards, but also carries a genuine downside: dying in combat hands the opponent a point. Both Champions reinforce the Legend's central "win a combat" trigger, but Audacious introduces real risk not present in Vanquisher or the Legend ability, making it the higher-variance option within the same combat-centric identity. Cross-referencing the table: Fury's pool leads in direct damage (17%) — Vanquisher's paid Might pump is a combat-power tool consistent with that aggression identity, though it's a stat pump rather than damage output per se. Chaos's pool leads in movement (24%) and trash-recursion (20%), but Audacious shows neither — its Deflect and point-on-win/point-on-death effects don't connect to either of Chaos's table-leading mechanics, another case worth stating plainly rather than forcing a link. Construction logic: a Legend built around consistently winning combats and converting those wins into repeated card draw, with the Champion choice deciding whether that plan additionally banks gear resources or gambles combat outcomes directly for points on both sides.

### Ezreal, Prodigal Explorer (Mind+Chaos)

Legend ability: `Exhaust: Reaction — Draw 1. Use only if you've chosen enemy units and/or gear twice this turn with spells or unit abilities.` **Ezreal, Prodigy** (Chaos): `When you play me, discard 1, then draw 2. Optional additional costs you pay cost 1 Energy or a rainbow rune less.` **Ezreal - Dashing** (Mind): `When I attack or defend, deal damage equal to my Might to an enemy unit here. I don't deal combat damage. A Mind rune: Action — Move me to your base.` The Legend ability is a Reaction gated behind a specific counting condition — targeting enemy units and/or gear twice in the same turn via spells or unit abilities — so it structurally demands multiple cheap, targeted interaction effects stacked into one turn before it pays off in a card. The two Champions split the job rather than pursue different plans: Dashing's attack/defend ability is itself a target-an-enemy-unit effect that can help satisfy the Legend's count while doubling as pseudo-removal (it deals ability damage, not combat damage, and can then retreat to base), while Prodigy is a pure enabler — discard/draw filtering and a discount on optional additional costs — that doesn't target enemies at all but smooths the resources needed to cast the spells that do. Cross-referencing the Domain table: Mind's highest categories are draw (17%), gear synergy (19%), and opponent-stat-weakening (12%) — the Legend's own draw payoff and its "choose enemy units/gear" condition both sit comfortably inside that stat-weakening/gear identity. Dashing's rune-cost retreat is also a small, notable counter to Mind's stated weakness of poor unit mobility (movement 7%, lowest of six). Chaos's table strength is movement/relocation (24%) and trash-recursion (20%), neither of which shows up in either Champion's text, so this Legend leans on Mind's identity far more than Chaos's — this is a Legend built around packing multiple cheap targeted effects on enemy units or gear into a single turn to fuel a recurring card-draw engine.

### Fiora, Grand Duelist (Body+Order)

Legend ability: `When one of your units becomes Mighty (5+ Might), you may exhaust me to channel 1 rune exhausted.` **Fiora - Peerless** (Body): `When I attack or defend one on one, double my Might this combat.` **Fiora - Worthy** (Order): `When a unit you control becomes Mighty (5+ Might), you may pay an Order rune to ready it.` The Legend ability fires immediately and repeatably off a stat threshold (any friendly unit reaching 5+ Might), converting that event into rune-channel resource ramp — this rewards a deck that can push units past 5 Might, and rewards it every time it happens, not just once. The two Champions reinforce each other tightly around that exact trigger: Peerless is a reliable way to cross the threshold (doubling Might in 1v1 combat), and Worthy generalizes the Legend's own condition into a second payoff — readying the newly-Mighty unit for a rune — so a single stat-crossing event can chain into rune-channel, readying, and (if it was Fiora herself) an already-doubled Might, all off the same trigger. Cross-referencing the table: Body's identity is explicitly the big-unit Domain (6+ Might units 27%, average Might 4.5, both highest of six), and Fiora's entire structure — multiple payoffs for crossing 5+ Might — is about as direct a match to that stated personality as exists in the pool. Order's identity is destroy-removal (32%) and death-benefit (12%); neither shows up in Worthy's text at all, so Order here functions as a tempo-support Domain layered onto a Body-driven stat-threshold plan rather than expressing its usual removal/sacrifice character — a Legend built around repeatedly pushing units past the Mighty threshold and cashing that single event in for compounding resource and tempo gains.

### Garen, Might of Demacia (Body+Order)

Legend ability: `When you conquer, if you have 4+ units at that battlefield, draw 2.` **Garen - Rugged** (Body): `Assault 2, Shield 2 (+2 Might while I'm an attacker or defender).` **Garen - Commander** (Order): `Other friendly units have +1 Might here.` The Legend ability triggers at the moment of conquest but is gated on a width condition already being true at that battlefield (4+ units), so it structurally rewards concentrating bodies at one location rather than spreading them thin or leaning on one or two big threats. The Champions diverge here: Commander's static +1 Might to every other friendly unit at its battlefield directly reinforces the Legend's own condition, since each additional unit stacked there both edges you toward the 4+ threshold and grows in raw stat payoff simultaneously; Rugged, by contrast, is a purely individual combat-stat bonus that doesn't care how many units share the battlefield, supporting a more self-contained, one-unit-wins-fights plan instead. Cross-referencing the table: Body's identity is big individual units (6+ Might 27%, average Might 4.5) — Rugged is a plain fit. Order's identity is destroy-removal (32%) and death-benefit (12%), and neither Champion touches removal or death triggers at all, so Order's usual table personality doesn't surface here; Commander's crowd-buff instead just services the Legend's own width condition, with no clear tie back to Order's stated strengths — a Legend built around massing multiple units at a single battlefield to both trigger and be rewarded by a conquer-draw condition.

### Jayce, Defender of Tomorrow (Mind+Body)

Cross-referencing the table: Mind's identity includes gear synergy at 19% (highest of six) — Brilliant Inventor's gear-turn trigger is a clean match. Body's identity is big units and high Might (6+ Might 27%, average Might 4.5); Hammer in Hand's keyword grant touches combat stats in that spirit but isn't specifically about raw size the way the table measures it. A Legend built around a gear-heavy deck that readies Equipment repeatedly to extend gear-ability uptime, doubling that engine once its one-time Empower cost is paid.

### Jinx - Loose Cannon (Fury+Chaos)

Construction logic: a Legend built around discarding cards as a fuel source rather than a cost, where an emptied hand each turn is the intended state, not an accident to avoid.

### Kha'Zix - Voidreaver (Body+Chaos)

Legend ability: `When you win a combat, gain 1 XP. Spend 1 XP, Exhaust: Buff a unit. Spend 2 XP, Exhaust: Move an exhausted friendly unit from a battlefield to its base.` Unusually, the entire generate-and-spend economy lives on the Legend card itself: a reactive trigger (combat win → XP) feeding two activated sinks of different cost (cheap buff vs. pricier retreat/repositioning). This structurally requires winning combats to function at all — a passive engine is worthless without fights happening. **Kha'Zix - Evolving Hunter** (Body) is `Hunt. When I conquer or hold, gain 1 XP. When I attack, you may spend 3 XP to deal damage equal to my Might to an enemy unit here` — a second, broader XP tap (conquer/hold, not just combat wins) plus its own high-cost sink that converts stockpiled XP into Might-scaled removal. **Kha'Zix - Mutating Horror** (Chaos) is `Ambush. When I attack or defend, if an enemy unit is alone here, give me +2 might this turn and gain 2 XP` — a third XP tap gated on facing an isolated target, with no sink of its own beyond the Legend's shared abilities.
