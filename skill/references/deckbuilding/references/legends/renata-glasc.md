### Renata Glasc - Chem-Baroness (Mind+Order)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — hextechanalytics.com and riftbound.cardsrealm.com's "Renata Combo" deck tech (2026-03-12) both confirm Renata Glasc, Mastermind as the real Chosen Champion and confirm the hold-into-Gold-token-into-direct-score win condition almost verbatim, down to the 4-Energy/4-Mind-rune payoff. One real divergence: Cardsrealm describes real decks scoring early via aggressive conquest with cheap units before transitioning into the hold-based plan, rather than establishing holds from the opening turns as the entry below describes, and labels the deck "control/combo" rather than pure control.

Legend ability: `When you or an ally hold, you may exhaust me to play a Gold gear token exhausted. While your score is within 3 points of the Victory Score, your Gold gear tokens add an additional Energy.` **Renata Glasc - Mastermind** (Mind): `1 Energy + a Mind rune: Draw 1. 4 Energy + 4 Mind runes, Exhaust: Score 1 point. Use my abilities only while I'm at a battlefield.` **Renata Glasc - Industrialist** (Order): `Your tokens enter ready.` (Real decks build around Mastermind as the Chosen Champion — see the callout above; neither real source treats Industrialist as a comparably load-bearing partner.)

Archetype: control (with real coverage adding "combo" for the Mastermind finishing sequence — see the callout above). The Legend's trigger is hold, not conquer — it rewards a battlefield you already possess and are sustaining, not the moment of capture, which is the reactive/conditional-on-persistence timing that signals a grind plan rather than a race. The scaling clause ("while your score is within 3 of Victory") is explicitly an endgame accelerant: the closer the game is to ending, the more Energy the deck's own gear economy produces, which only matters to a deck planning to still be playing a long game at that point.

Core unfolding: this entry reads the early game as being about planting units and establishing holds rather than forcing fights — every turn spent holding mints another Gold gear token, and Industrialist makes that token (and any other tokens) enter ready instead of exhausted, so its resource-adding reaction is live the same turn it appears. Real coverage nuances this: at least one real competitive list scores its first points by conquering battlefields early with cheap units, only transitioning to the hold-based plan once ahead. Once the engine is running, Mastermind sits at a battlefield converting banked Mind runes into a steady card-draw drip, then, once enough Energy and rune investment has accumulated, can pay a large activation cost to score a point directly — a second, combat-independent path to points layered on top of holding, and real decks close games by chaining Time Warp with Mastermind's score-a-point activation in the same turn. As the game nears the Victory Score, the Legend's own bonus Energy from Gold gear compounds, making the late-game turns where this deck is strongest exactly the turns it was built to reach.

Win condition: primarily holding uncontested to keep the hold-trigger firing and the gear economy running, with Mastermind offering a genuine secondary path of spending accumulated resources to score directly rather than through combat or capture at all — an unusually explicit resource-to-points conversion for a control shell, and real competitive coverage confirms this exact closing sequence rather than treating it as a text-only hypothesis.
