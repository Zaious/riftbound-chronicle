### Jinx - Loose Cannon (Fury+Chaos)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — the aggressive, discard-driven shape is real (one real source calls it a "tempo-control hybrid with discard synergies," close enough to "aggro" to count as confirmed), but this entry implicitly treated Demolitionist as the default Champion, and that's backwards: real coverage states Demolitionist "lost some appeal" once Spiritforged shipped a better 3-cost payoff (Ezreal, Prodigy), and that "Rebel is more popular" since — a real tournament Top 8 write-up names Rebel, not Demolitionist, as the deck's "primary battlefield threat." Also worth flagging: this archetype is currently off-meta (bottom tier, ~0% metashare in the current set) despite a real Top-8 result in an earlier set — treat any claim about it as describing a real but currently-dormant line, not an active top performer.

Legend ability: `At start of your Beginning Phase, draw 1 if you have one or fewer cards in your hand.` **Jinx - Demolitionist** (Fury): `Accelerate. Assault 2. When you play me, discard 2.` **Jinx - Rebel** (Chaos): `When you discard one or more cards, ready me and give me +1 might this turn.`

**Archetype**: Aggro/tempo-hybrid, discard-driven. The Legend ability fires at the start of your turn but is conditioned on already holding one or fewer cards — it doesn't build toward anything over multiple turns, it simply refuses to let an emptied hand run dry, which only matters if the deck's plan is to empty its hand fast in the first place. Demolitionist enters ready via Accelerate (immediate board presence) and discards 2 the moment it's played — an aggressive tempo play that also actively pushes hand size toward the Legend's draw condition — but real practice has favored Rebel as the primary Champion since Spiritforged, not Demolitionist. Rebel converts any discard into an immediate ready-plus-buff, and real coverage frames it as pushing the deck toward "a slower, more combo oriented strategy, able to score multiple points per turn" rather than just "extra combat actions" the way a pure text read suggests.

**Core unfolding**: Early turns want to dump the hand as completely as possible — Demolitionist's forced discard-2-on-play does this by itself while putting an Accelerate, Assault-2 attacker on board same-turn. Once the loop is running, near-empty hands trigger the guaranteed draw every Beginning Phase, so the deck never actually runs out of gas despite playing everything each turn; Rebel turns that same discard habit into extra combat actions (ready + buff) rather than a pure cost. There's no delayed payoff here — every piece resolves the turn it's used, and the deck closes out by simply attacking every turn with a refreshed hand and buffed/readied bodies, not by assembling a single big swing.

**Engine to points**: Assault-buffed, discard-readied units are meant to win Showdowns outright turn after turn — this is a combat-attrition aggro plan, and the draw-on-empty-hand exists purely to keep that attack pattern from stalling, not to build independent card advantage.
