### Vi, Piltover Enforcer (Fury+Order)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, leaning NOT CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — the aggro/conquer shape is real, but "both halves agreed" overstates parity: real tournament decklists overwhelmingly run Peacekeeper, and Hotheaded appears in zero of the sampled real Regional Qualifier lists (several ran neither named Champion at all, using an older base-set Vi card instead). Play-rate data at scale confirms the lean — Peacekeeper vastly outpaces Hotheaded, though both are mostly played as generic splashes into *other* Legends rather than in Vi's own deck. This Legend is also currently weak overall: bottom tier, near-0% metashare, sub-50% recorded win rate.

Legend ability: `When you conquer, if you assigned 3 or more excess damage, you may exhaust me to ready a unit.` **Vi, Hotheaded** (Fury): `Deflect. 2 Energy and a Fury rune: Double my Might this turn.` **Vi, Peacekeeper** (Order): `Ambush. When I attack, Stun an enemy unit here.`

The Legend trigger fires on conquer, conditioned on overkill (3+ excess damage), and its payoff is immediate: readying a unit for a second action the same turn. This is a burst-tempo mechanic, not a compounding one — it rewards a single decisive combat that overshoots, then refunds that tempo instantly. On paper, both Champions feed the same overkill condition rather than diverging — but real deckbuilding leans hard on Peacekeeper (Ambush plus attack-triggered Stun to clear the blocker standing between Vi and an uncontested conquer) over Hotheaded (activated Might-doubling), which barely sees play in practice. Treat Peacekeeper as the real, if still weak, default rather than a coequal option.

Core unfolding: deploy Vi early and get her into combat as soon as the board allows — Peacekeeper's Ambush lets her jump straight into a battlefield reactively rather than waiting a full turn to develop there. Once she connects, Hotheaded's Might-double (or simply a big enough stat line) pushes damage well past what's needed to win the Showdown, tripping the Legend's overkill condition; the readied unit that results can then immediately attack or defend again, turning one combat into two actions within a single turn. This is a within-turn snowball rather than a turn-over-turn one — the deck's ceiling is how many conquer-then-ready chains it can string together in one attack phase. It converts into points by winning Showdowns outright and then using the freed action to either conquer a second Battlefield or defend a held one immediately after, pressing the advantage before the opponent can stabilize.
