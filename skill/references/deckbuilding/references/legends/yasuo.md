### Yasuo, Unforgiven (Calm+Chaos)

Legend ability: `2 Energy, Exhaust: Move a friendly unit to or from its base.` **Yasuo, Remorseful** (Calm): `When I attack, deal damage equal to my Might to an enemy unit here.` **Yasuo, Windrider** (Chaos): `Ganking. The third time I move in a turn, you score 1 point.`

The Legend ability is a flexible, repeatable mobility tool with no combat condition attached — the shared resource across the kit is movement itself. The two Champions convert that shared resource into different win paths. Remorseful is a combat trick: attacking deals damage equal to Might to a blocker, directly answering whatever's in the way — this is aggro, clearing the path to attack again. Windrider is structurally different from anything else in this batch: "the third time I move in a turn, you score 1 point" is a scoring trigger entirely independent of combat or end-of-turn holds — it converts accumulated movement, within a single turn, directly into a point. That is a combo payoff by the game's own definition (a patient setup that detonates a turn swinging a point at once), not an aggro or control mechanic.

Core unfolding with Windrider: Ganking gives Yasuo one free battlefield-to-battlefield move on his own; the Legend ability can supply a second and, if paid for twice, a third move-to/from-base in the same turn — so the deck needs enough spare Energy after its other plays to activate the Legend ability more than once to hit the third-move threshold. Once that's consistently achievable, every such turn scores a bonus point directly from the ability text — a recurring, non-combat scoring engine layered on top of the normal win condition rather than replacing it. With Remorseful instead, the deck is a straightforward aggro shell: attack early, use the Might-scaling damage to clear blockers turn after turn, and use the Legend's move-to-base defensively to dodge unfavorable combat or offensively to bring a fresh attacker in — converting into points by winning Showdowns outright.
