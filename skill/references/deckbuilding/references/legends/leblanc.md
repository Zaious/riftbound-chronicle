### LeBlanc - Deceiver (Mind+Order)

> Errata-corrected 2026-08-17 (`references/errata_overlay.json`, Unleashed Errata Updates, 2026-04-03) — the Legend ability text below reflects the official wording fix. No mechanical change from the pre-errata text; the analysis is unaffected.

> Tier 2-confirmed NOT CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — real coverage (hextechanalytics.com, riftbound.cardsrealm.com) names this a Combo/Value archetype, "Deathknell LeBlanc," built around repeatedly triggering Fragmented's Deathknell and doubling it with Karthus, Eternal (OGN-236) for exponential card draw — the opposite of the Control read below, and explicitly a card-advantage engine rather than the net-card-loss attrition tool this entry describes. Fragmented is confirmed as the real "primary chosen Champion," with Everywhere At Once a real but secondary/flex inclusion, not a co-equal defensive partner.

Legend ability: `When you conquer or hold, you may discard 1 and exhaust me to play a ready Reflection unit token there. Then do this: It becomes a copy of another unit there. Give it Temporary.` **LeBlanc - Everywhere At Once** (Mind): `Backline. Your Temporary effects at my battlefield don't trigger.` **LeBlanc - Fragmented** (Order): `Assault. Deathknell — Draw 1. If it's your Beginning Phase, draw 2 instead.` (Real decks run Fragmented as the primary Champion — see the callout above; Everywhere At Once sees real but secondary play, not an equal defensive-shield partner.)

**Archetype**: Text alone reads as Control — the Legend's trigger requires you to already be conquering or holding, which looks like a reward layered on top of already winning the core objective rather than a way to establish a first foothold, the shape of a hold-and-grind plan. Real competitive coverage describes something different: a Combo/Value deck nicknamed "Deathknell LeBlanc." The engine is built to *want* its own Reflection tokens (and other Deathknell units) to die — the Legend's Temporary clone dying at the next Beginning Phase is the trigger, not an unfortunate side effect — and Fragmented's Deathknell turns every one of those deaths into a card, or two during your own Beginning Phase. Karthus, Eternal (`Your DEATHKNELL effects trigger an additional time`) doubles that output, and discard/sacrifice fodder (Sacrifice, Facebreaker, Black Rose Dignitary) exists specifically to feed more Deathknell triggers, not just to fund the Legend's activation cost.

**Core unfolding**: Because the engine only activates once you've already conquered or held, the early game is about landing a first foothold and getting draw sources like Watchful Sentry and Karthus, Eternal down early. Once running, real decks don't treat the Reflection clone as merely "an extra ready body" — its scheduled death is the payoff, converted into card draw via Fragmented's Deathknell and doubled by Karthus. The closing plan is a resource-generation loop (real coverage describes "generating a lot of cards and a lot of runes") that outpaces the opponent on cards while still applying pressure with the temporary clones, rather than a purely defensive re-fortification of the same battlefields.

**Engine to points**: The text-only read that this requires holding, and that holding funds the follow-up Showdowns needed to retain a battlefield, is directionally fine at the mechanical level. But the claim that it "is not a card-advantage engine" is contradicted directly by real practice — the entire competitive shell exists to convert Deathknell deaths into card draw, doubled by Karthus, Eternal, making resource advantage (not attrition) the actual engine funding the win.
