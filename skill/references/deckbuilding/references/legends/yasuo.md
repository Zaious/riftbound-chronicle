### Yasuo, Unforgiven (Calm+Chaos)

> Tier 2-confirmed NOT CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — not a real aggro/combo fork; there is effectively no fork at all. Real coverage states Remorseful "completely disappeared" from the format, eclipsed by better Champions elsewhere in the format (Draven - Audacious, Irelia - Fervent) — Windrider simply won the one Champion slot outright. More strikingly, the surviving competitive Yasuo build isn't built around Windrider's own printed payoff either: it's described directly as "almost a perfect copy" of a *different* Legend's deck (Irelia's snowball/protect-the-threat shell), with Windrider's move-3-times-for-a-point bonus layered on top of that borrowed plan rather than being the plan itself. This is a case where Tier 1's text-only reading (aggro vs. combo, split by Champion) doesn't just get the split wrong — it misses that the winning archetype isn't built around either Champion's own text at all. Overall competitive framing: "mostly played for fun, or to earn a best-of card," not a serious contender.

Legend ability: `2 Energy, Exhaust: Move a friendly unit to or from its base.` **Yasuo, Remorseful** (Calm): `When I attack, deal damage equal to my Might to an enemy unit here.` **Yasuo, Windrider** (Chaos): `Ganking. The third time I move in a turn, you score 1 point.`

The Legend ability is a flexible, repeatable mobility tool with no combat condition attached — the shared resource across the kit is movement itself. On paper, Remorseful (a combat trick, Might-scaling removal) and Windrider (a combo-shaped move-three-times bonus point) read as pulling toward different archetypes. Real practice doesn't support that split, though: Remorseful isn't played — Windrider holds the only Champion slot that sees play — and even the Windrider list isn't built around the printed "third move scores a point" payoff as its core plan. What's actually played is a snowball/protection shell borrowed from a structurally similar, better-supported Legend (Irelia): threats that grow and get protected turn over turn (community-cited: Stellacorn Herder, Guardian Angel, Zhonya's Hourglass), with Yasuo's own move-triggered bonus point riding along as a secondary payoff rather than the deck's identity.

**Core unfolding:** early game establishes and protects a snowballing threat the same way an Irelia-style deck would — this Legend's own printed kit (the Legend ability's move-to/from-base, Windrider's Ganking and move-count bonus) supports that plan opportunistically (repositioning a threatened unit, occasionally cashing in a bonus point off incidental movement) rather than defining it. **Win condition:** primarily winning Showdowns outright with a protected, grown threat — the same route a borrowed archetype takes — with Windrider's bonus point as an occasional extra rather than a dedicated combo turn. Treat any Tier 1 reading of this Legend's own kit with real skepticism: this is the clearest case so far of real practice not building around the printed Legend+Champion text at all.
