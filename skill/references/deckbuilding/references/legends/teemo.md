### Teemo, Swift Scout (Mind+Chaos)

Legend ability: `You may pay 1 Energy to hide a card with Hidden instead of a rainbow rune. 1 Energy, Exhaust: Put a Teemo unit you own into your hand from your Champion Zone or the board.` **Teemo, Strategist** (Mind): `Hidden... When I defend or I'm played from Hidden, reveal the top 5 cards of your Main Deck. Deal 1 to an enemy unit here for each card with Hidden, then recycle them.` **Teemo, Scout** (Chaos): `Hidden... When you play me, give me +3 might this turn.`

The Legend ability is an enabler, not a payoff: it makes Hidden cheaper to activate and lets you recycle a Teemo body back to hand for repeated re-deployment. That recursion is archetype-neutral by itself — what it feeds differs sharply by Champion. Strategist's trigger is reactive (defend, or resolving from Hidden) and its payoff scales with how many Hidden cards are stacked in the deck at reveal time — this is a control tool: sit back, let Teemo defend or flip up on your own schedule, and punish whatever's attacking. Scout's trigger is immediate-on-play, a flat +3 tempo burst with no dependency on deck state — this is straightforward aggro pressure. The two Champions genuinely pull toward different lanes off the same chassis; the Legend ability's retrieval loop just lets whichever plan you pick reuse the same unit's trigger multiple times rather than deciding the lane itself.

Core unfolding depends on the pick. With Strategist, the deck wants a critical mass of Hidden cards seeded early (cheap via the Legend's energy-alternative payment) so that by the time Teemo defends or unhides, the top-5 reveal is dense with Hidden hits — a grind that improves as the game goes on, converting into points by surviving attacks and holding Battlefields rather than pushing them. With Scout, the deck wants Teemo hidden and popped up as fast as possible for the +3 might spike, then the retrieval ability recurs him to do it again — converting into points by winning individual Showdowns outright through repeated burst attacks, racing before the opponent's board stabilizes.
