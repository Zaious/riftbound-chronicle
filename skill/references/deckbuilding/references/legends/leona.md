### Leona - Radiant Dawn (Calm+Order)

Legend ability: `When you stun one or more enemy units, buff a friendly unit.` **Leona - Zealot** (Calm): `If an opponent's score is within 3 points of the Victory Score, I enter ready. Stunned enemy units here have -8 might, to a minimum of 1 might.` **Leona, Determined** (Order): `Shield. When I attack, stun an enemy unit here.`

**Archetype**: Control. Every piece keys off disruption rather than racing or a delayed combo. The Legend converts each stun landed into an accumulating buff for your own side — a reactive trigger tied to a defensive/tempo tool (stunning neutralizes a unit for the turn), not a burst or end-of-turn payoff. Determined generates that stun immediately on attack, meaning the deck initiates fights specifically to neutralize a blocker while also feeding its own buff engine in the same action. Zealot is explicitly a comeback tool — she's dead weight early (her ready-on-play only triggers when the opponent is within 3 of Victory Score) and devastating late, turning any stun at her battlefield into a near-total -8 Might shutdown. That conditional design itself signals a grinding, come-from-behind archetype rather than an early-committing one.

**Core unfolding**: Early game wants Determined attacking to start generating stuns, locking down defenders one fight at a time while your own units grow stronger from the resulting buffs. As the game develops, the board tilts further with every Showdown initiated, since stunned enemies both can't contribute to that fight and are actively fueling your side's stat growth. In the endgame specifically, once the opponent nears the Victory Score, Zealot comes online as a dedicated defensive answer — entering ready for free and converting any stun at her location into a near-complete neutralization of the threatening unit.

**Engine to points**: A hold/win-via-disruption plan — stunning removes the opponent's ability to contest the current Showdown, accumulated buffs make your units progressively better at winning the next ones, and Zealot exists specifically to convert that disruption into holding a battlefield exactly when the game is on the line. No card-advantage or single-turn-combo signal anywhere in the kit.
