### Azir - Emperor of the Sands (Calm+Order)

Legend ability: `Your Sand Soldiers have Weaponmaster. 1 Energy, Exhaust: Play a 2 Might Sand Soldier unit token to your base. Use only if you've played an Equipment this turn.`
Azir - Ascendant (Calm): `A Calm rune: Action — Choose a unit you control. Move me to its location and it to my original location. If it's equipped, you may attach one of its Equipment to me. Use only once per turn.`
Azir - Sovereign (Order): `Accelerate (...) When I attack, you may move any number of your token units to this battlefield.`

**Archetype:** The two Champions pull in genuinely different directions and that split is worth stating plainly rather than forcing a single label. The Legend ability is a repeatable, condition-gated engine — every turn you play an Equipment, you can spend energy to add another 2-might Sand Soldier token — which builds a wide board gradually over many turns, not in one burst. Sovereign then supplies a combo-shaped payoff: `When I attack, you may move any number of your token units to this battlefield` collapses a slowly-accumulated, dispersed token army onto a single Showdown, converting patient setup into one decisive swing. Ascendant, by contrast, is a positional utility tool — swap places with a unit and optionally steal its Equipment — that supports steadier, incremental equipment value and protecting key pieces rather than any single climactic turn; it reads as control/grind-adjacent, with no scoring text of its own.

**Core unfolding:** Early game requires playing an Equipment every turn just to keep the token engine online at all — that's a hard gate, not a suggestion, since the token-play ability explicitly does nothing without an Equipment played that same turn. With Sovereign, the deck spends several turns quietly building a wide, individually unthreatening board of 2-might tokens across multiple battlefields, then uses Sovereign's attack trigger to mass-mobilize all of them onto one contested battlefield at once, turning an otherwise unremarkable token spread into an overwhelming single Showdown. With Ascendant instead, the same token/Equipment base is used more defensively — repositioning to dodge removal aimed at a key equipped unit and consolidating Equipment onto Azir himself.

**Win condition:** With Sovereign, this is a genuine combo line: many turns of quiet token accumulation cashed in on one attack to win a Showdown outright through sudden numbers. With Ascendant, the text supports protecting equipped value and steady board presence, but nothing in her line states a direct scoring mechanism, so her contribution to actually converting into points is the weaker-supported half of this Legend's kit and shouldn't be overstated.
