### Mel - Soul's Reflection (Mind+Chaos)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — the denial-engine mechanic (a repeatable, resource-gated Might-shrink that swings one chosen Showdown rather than detonating in a single burst turn) is strongly corroborated almost verbatim by hextechanalytics.com, which calls Mel a "setup-control Legend" whose "deck wins by making one prepared fight inefficient for the opponent." Mel, Newly Awakened is confirmed the real "most common chosen Champion" (hextechanalytics), roughly matching this entry's read of both Champions reinforcing one identity — Defiant Soul is real but secondary, not unplayed. Two real sources disagree on the top-line archetype label, though: hextechanalytics and riftboundguide.com (pre-launch) call it Control; riftbound.gg's own post-launch guide (updated 2026-08-08) calls it "midrange, value oriented" instead — reported here as a genuine disagreement rather than resolved into one label. riftDecks.com data also surfaces a nuance this entry didn't anticipate: Newly Awakened, while the more commonly run Champion (54.42% presence), is tagged a below-average-performing TRAP card (0.80x top-cut rate), while the less-played Defiant Soul (29.25% presence) performs close to the deck's own average (0.97x).

Legend ability: `When you empower something else, empower me. (I become Empowered if I'm not already.) Disempower me, Exhaust: Give a unit at a battlefield -2 Might this turn.` **Mel, Newly Awakened** (Mind): `When you play me, draw 1. Empower 3 Energy. Empowered: Your spells and abilities can't be countered. If a spell or ability you control would give -Might to a unit it chooses, it gives an additional -1 Might.` **Mel, Defiant Soul** (Chaos): `Empower — Discard a spell. When I become Empowered, banish an enemy unit at a battlefield with 3 Might or less.`

The Legend's own trigger is reactive and derivative — it doesn't fire on its own timing, it piggybacks on Empower events happening elsewhere in the deck ("when you empower something else"), meaning Mel's payoff scales with how many Empower-capable pieces the deck runs, not with any single big turn. The payoff itself, disempowering Mel to shave -2 might off an enemy unit, is a repeatable removal-adjacent tool, not a burst finisher. Both Champions confirm a denial-engine reading rather than a race: Newly Awakened's Empowered state protects the deck's spells/abilities from being countered and adds an extra -1 might onto any -might effect already being applied — amplifying the shrink-based removal rather than replacing it with damage or destroy effects. Defiant Soul pays for its own Empower by discarding a spell (a real resource cost, not free) and its payoff for becoming Empowered is an outright banish of a small enemy unit (3 might or less) — conditional, cheap-target removal rather than an unconditional sweep. None of this compounds into a single explosive turn; it's a steady, multi-piece engine that needs several Empower triggers across a game to keep firing, which is a control texture (patient denial over time) with a combo-adjacent setup requirement (needs synergy pieces already assembled to function at all) rather than a genuine combo detonation. Real coverage backs this "control texture" read directly (hextechanalytics.com calls Mel a "setup-control Legend"), though a separate, more recent source (riftbound.gg) instead labels the same deck "midrange, value oriented" — the two real sources don't agree on the label even though they describe the same shrink-and-punish plan. Newly Awakened is confirmed the real, more commonly played Champion, but real play-rate data also flags her as a card that correlates with slightly worse tournament finishes than the Legend's own average, while the less-played Defiant Soul performs closer to average — a wrinkle this text-only derivation had no way to see. Points: this deck doesn't push its own units forward to conquer so much as it shrinks and picks off the opponent's small units so that Showdowns the opponent should win are lost instead, letting friendly Battlefields go uncontested or letting cheap units hold ground that would otherwise be overrun.
