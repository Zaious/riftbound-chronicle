### Sett - The Boss (Body+Order)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — the Brawler/Kingpin Champion split is unusually real for once (riftDecks.com: Brawler FLEX ~50%, Kingpin FLEX ~41%, no dominant default), but the archetype label has drifted with the meta: current coverage (riftbound.gg, updated 2026-07-07) describes the deck as aggressive — "score at all costs," "extremely aggressively" — rather than control/midrange, and two independent sources across two different eras (Cardsrealm 2025-12-06 and riftbound.gg 2026-07-07) both name **Fiora, Victorious**, a card entirely outside Sett's own kit, as the deck's actual main win condition, not either printed Champion's buff mechanic. Sett currently sits Tier 5 and declining on riftbound.gg's August 2026 tier list.

Legend ability: `When a buffed unit you control would die, you may pay a rainbow rune and exhaust me to spend its buff and recall it exhausted instead. When you conquer, ready me.` **Sett, Brawler** (Body): `When you play me or when I conquer, buff me. Spend my buff: Give me +4 might this turn.` **Sett - Kingpin** (Order): `Tank. I get +1 might for each buffed friendly unit at my battlefield.`

Archetype: control/midrange, with a combo-adjacent option inside Brawler — a text-derived read that matches how the deck was described in the earlier Origins-format meta ("a midrange value-oriented deck... looking to win the game through attrition," riftbound.gg, Feb 2026) but not the current one. Current real coverage instead frames it as a proactive rush deck: "a 'Score at all costs' type of legend," one that "isn't suited for a value oriented war" and needs to "constantly be in a sort of rush, and score every turn" (riftbound.gg, updated 2026-07-07) — closer to aggro-with-a-resilience-toolkit than to control. The Legend's core mechanic is a reactive death-insurance clause — it doesn't fire on a schedule, it fires conditionally whenever a buffed unit would otherwise be lost, converting a bad combat trade into merely spending a buff and a rune to recall the unit instead. That's a resilience mechanic, not a race mechanic on its own: it exists so the deck can keep re-fighting with the same accumulated investment rather than bleeding board state, but real play uses that resilience in service of staying ahead on tempo and racing to score, not to grind out a control war. The conquer clause (ready Sett) reinforces that this insurance stays available across repeated engagements rather than being a one-time save.

Brawler buffs itself on play or conquer and can later cash that buff in for a one-turn +4 might spike — a bank-now, detonate-later pattern that swings a single Showdown decisively, the deck's one genuinely combo-flavored piece. Kingpin instead scales passively off the number of buffed friendly units already sitting at its battlefield, rewarding clustering multiple buffed bodies together rather than banking one big spender, and Tank means it (not its buffed companions) absorbs damage first. Unlike almost every other Legend checked so far, this really is close to a genuine, roughly-even split in real decks rather than one Champion dominating — riftDecks.com shows Brawler and Kingpin at comparable, non-overlapping presence (roughly 50%/41%, both only FLEX rather than CORE), and riftbound.gg notes "the chosen champion can be Sett - Brawler in some decks" rather than treating either as the sole default. **What neither Champion is, in real play, is the deck's actual win condition** — two independent sources, three months apart, both single out **Fiora, Victorious** (not a Sett card at all) as that: "Fiora - Victorious is perhaps the most important card in this list... She is your main win condition, in fact" (Cardsrealm, 2025-12-06); "Fiora - Victorious leads the way... making her the perfect turn 2 play" (riftbound.gg, 2026-07-07). riftDecks.com confirms she's the single highest-presence card in the deck (CORE, 77.27%), ahead of both Sett Champions.

Core unfolding: early game is about getting units buffed (via Brawler's play/conquer trigger) rather than winning fights outright. Mid-game, the Legend's recall-instead-of-die clause is what lets the deck keep committing those buffed units to combat repeatedly without attrition costing it the board — this is a real, corroborated engine, but real lists lean on it to protect a proactive scoring push rather than to wage a patient control war. Closing out, Brawler spends its banked buff for a sudden four-might swing to force a Showdown win, while Kingpin's payoff is a slow-built, hard-to-crack stack of buffed defenders at one battlefield — but in practice the card doing the actual closing is usually the off-kit Fiora, Victorious, not either Champion.

Win condition: the text-only read is holding via resilience — buffed threats that would die get recalled instead, keeping a defended battlefield defended turn after turn — with Brawler layering in an outright-win-the-Showdown burst option on top of that base plan. Real coverage names a different, off-kit win condition instead (Fiora, Victorious's resource generation off a +1 might buff), and describes the overall plan as racing to score every turn rather than settling into a defended hold.
