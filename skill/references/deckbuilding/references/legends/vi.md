### Vi, Piltover Enforcer (Fury+Order)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, leaning NOT CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — the aggro/conquer shape is real, but "both halves agreed" overstates parity: real tournament decklists overwhelmingly run Peacekeeper, and Hotheaded appears in zero of the sampled real Regional Qualifier lists (several ran neither named Champion at all, using an older base-set Vi card instead). Play-rate data at scale confirms the lean — Peacekeeper vastly outpaces Hotheaded, though both are mostly played as generic splashes into *other* Legends rather than in Vi's own deck. This Legend is also currently weak overall: bottom tier, near-0% metashare, sub-50% recorded win rate.

> Follow-up 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — a source-data pipeline bug (fixed in `extract_legend_packets.py`) meant this entry never considered **Vi, Destructive** (Fury, `ogn-036-298`, reprinted `ven-167-166`), an older Origins-base-set print sharing Hotheaded's Fury Domain. This is, in fact, the specific "older base-set Vi card" the callout above already referenced without naming it: riftDecks.com's own Vi legend stats page shows Destructive FLEX at 16.13% presence in Vi's own decks — roughly five times Hotheaded's FRINGE 3.23% — and a real Sydney Regional Qualifier decklist (one of the four this file already sampled) runs it directly as Chosen Champion. It doesn't unseat Peacekeeper as the real, if weak, overall default, but it does mean Hotheaded — the Fury print this entry actually analyzed — is the least-played of Vi's three real Champion prints, not merely "barely played" relative to one rival.

Legend ability: `When you conquer, if you assigned 3 or more excess damage, you may exhaust me to ready a unit.` **Vi, Hotheaded** (Fury): `Deflect. 2 Energy and a Fury rune: Double my Might this turn.` **Vi, Peacekeeper** (Order): `Ambush. When I attack, Stun an enemy unit here.` A third real print a source-data bug kept out of this entry's original analysis, sharing Hotheaded's Fury Domain: **Vi, Destructive** (Fury): `Ganking. Recycle 1 from your trash: Give me +1 Might this turn.`

The Legend trigger fires on conquer, conditioned on overkill (3+ excess damage), and its payoff is immediate: readying a unit for a second action the same turn. This is a burst-tempo mechanic, not a compounding one — it rewards a single decisive combat that overshoots, then refunds that tempo instantly. On paper, all three Champions feed the same overkill condition rather than diverging — but real deckbuilding leans hard on Peacekeeper (Ambush plus attack-triggered Stun to clear the blocker standing between Vi and an uncontested conquer) over either Fury print. Between the two Fury options, Destructive (Ganking plus a trash-recycling Might boost) is real-if-niche and actually outplays Hotheaded (activated Might-doubling) in Vi's own decks, per riftDecks.com's play-rate data — Hotheaded is the least-played of the three, not just the second choice behind Peacekeeper. Treat Peacekeeper as the real, if still weak, default, with Destructive as the occasional real Fury-side substitute and Hotheaded essentially unplayed.

Core unfolding: deploy Vi early and get her into combat as soon as the board allows — Peacekeeper's Ambush lets her jump straight into a battlefield reactively rather than waiting a full turn to develop there (Destructive's Ganking offers the same battlefield-to-battlefield mobility on the rare list that runs it instead). Once she connects, Hotheaded's Might-double, Destructive's Recycle-fueled Might boost, or simply a big enough stat line pushes damage well past what's needed to win the Showdown, tripping the Legend's overkill condition; the readied unit that results can then immediately attack or defend again, turning one combat into two actions within a single turn. This is a within-turn snowball rather than a turn-over-turn one — the deck's ceiling is how many conquer-then-ready chains it can string together in one attack phase. It converts into points by winning Showdowns outright and then using the freed action to either conquer a second Battlefield or defend a held one immediately after, pressing the advantage before the opponent can stabilize.
