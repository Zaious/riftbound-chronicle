### Lux - Lady of Luminosity (Mind+Order)

Legend ability: `When you play a spell that costs 5 Energy or more, draw 1.` **Lux, Crownguard** (Order): `Exhaust: Reaction — Add 2 Energy. Spend this Energy only to play spells.` **Lux - Illuminated** (Mind): `When you play a spell that costs 5 Energy or more, give me +3 Might this turn.`

The Legend trigger fires immediately on-play, not end-of-turn or conditionally-delayed, but it's gated behind a real cost threshold (5+ Energy) rather than any spell — so the deck isn't rewarded for flooding cheap interaction, it's rewarded for actually landing its expensive spells and getting paid the instant it does. Crownguard's Reaction adds Energy earmarked only for spells, at instant speed, which reads as resource-smoothing to make sure that 5-cost threshold is reachable on curve or held up defensively rather than only on a dedicated big-spell turn. Illuminated redirects the same trigger into an immediate +3 might on Lux herself, converting card advantage into combat relevance in the same turn. This is a control/midrange spell-payoff shell: the plan isn't to race early, it's to reach the point where each big spell is efficient (replaces itself via draw) while Crownguard keeps Energy available reactively so the deck isn't caught flat-footed holding up an answer. Early turns are about surviving to the 5-cost spells, not pressuring; once the engine is live, each big spell played is a two-for-one (the spell's own effect plus a card plus, with Illuminated, a temporary threat on Lux). It doesn't detonate multiple points at once the way a combo turn would — each trigger is a single, repeatable value swing, not a stored-up alpha strike — so this sits closer to control than combo despite the burst-shaped individual trigger. Points: the draw side sustains card advantage that lets the deck keep answering and holding Battlefields over a long game (control's usual path), while Illuminated gives it a secondary lever to actually win a Showdown outright on the turn a big spell lands, rather than depending purely on attrition.
