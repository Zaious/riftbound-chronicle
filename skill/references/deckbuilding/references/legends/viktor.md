### Viktor, Herald of the Arcane (Mind+Order)

> Tier 2-confirmed 2026-08-17 (`references/verification-log.md`) — real coverage uses "off-tempo," a compatible but different label than "control." More importantly: competitive lists skew heavily toward one Champion (Leader/Order) rather than treating both as comparably played — the original entry presented Innovator and Leader as parallel sub-styles, which overstates how evenly this Legend's real builds actually split.

Legend ability: `1 Energy, Exhaust: Play a 1 might Recruit unit token.` **Viktor, Innovator** (Mind): `When you play a card on an opponent's turn, play a 1 might Recruit unit token in your base.` **Viktor, Leader** (Order): `When another non-Recruit unit you control dies, play a 1 might Recruit unit token into your base.`

All three token sources share one property: none of them require winning combat or conquering to trigger. The Legend ability is a flat, repeatable resource conversion (energy into a body) available every turn regardless of board state; Leader rewards your own units dying, converting combat losses the deck was going to take anyway into board refill. This is steady, incremental board replenishment rather than a burst plan — real coverage's "off-tempo" label fits that shape: this Legend isn't racing to be efficient on any given turn, it's playing a longer game where it can afford to look inefficient early because the token engine keeps paying it back. Innovator's reward for playing cards reactively on the opponent's turn is textually plausible but is the less-played real option — treat Leader as this Legend's primary, established identity and Innovator as the less-tested alternative, not a coequal second lane.

**Core unfolding:** from turn one, spend the Legend ability's energy-exhaust every available turn to drip a 1-might Recruit into your base — this is the deck's floor, not a bonus, since it needs no other card in play. With Leader (the real-practice default), the plan leans into letting bigger units trade away in combat rather than avoiding those trades, since every death refills the board — this Legend can afford fights other decks would consider bad value. The mid-to-late game is a battlefield full of cheap, replaceable bodies rather than a small number of large threats. It converts into points by holding many Battlefields with a constantly replenished supply of small units over time — not by winning any individual Showdown decisively, but by making sure there's always another body ready to recontest one that's lost.
