Legend ability: `1 Energy, Exhaust: Return a friendly unit at a battlefield to its owner's hand. Play a Gold gear token exhausted.` **Pyke - Dockside Butcher** (Fury): `Hidden. Ganking. You may pay a Fury rune as an additional cost to play me. When you play me, if you paid the additional cost, ready me and give me +2 Might this turn.` **Pyke - Returned** (Chaos): `Hidden. Backline. Once each turn, when an enemy unit dies while I'm at a battlefield, play a Gold gear token exhausted.`

The Legend ability is a flexible, activated bounce: return a friendly unit from a Battlefield to hand and bank a Gold gear token that can later be cracked for a rainbow rune — a tempo tool with no single fixed direction. Both Champions carry Hidden, a surprise-timing mechanic that's inherently about ambush tempo rather than telegraphed commitment. On paper, Dockside Butcher (immediate, aggressive, ready-plus-buff-on-play) and Returned (patient, protected, passive resource generation off enemy deaths) read as a clean aggro/control fork — but real deckbuilding doesn't sort along that axis. The real "Disrupt Control" build described by actual coverage still runs Dockside Butcher as its Chosen Champion, deployed as a *late-game closer* inside an otherwise attrition-focused, Chaos-leaning shell — "surprise the opponent with Dockside Butcher, then close out the game." The lane that actually varies between real Pyke lists is the Fury/Chaos rune split (more aggressive vs. more disruptive), independent of which Champion is in the box.
