### Irelia - Blade Dancer (Calm+Chaos)

> Tier 2-confirmed NOT CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 for the combo/single-turn-swing framing (`references/verification-log.md`), though the underlying Legend mechanics below are read correctly and this Legend's real competitive strength (Tier 1 on the current meta list) is confirmed. Irelia, Fervent is CORE at 75.99% real presence; **Irelia, Graceful does not appear anywhere in riftDecks.com's 86 tracked cards for this Legend** — she is not a real symmetric combo partner, she's essentially unplayed. Two independent deep-dive real guides frame the win condition as the opposite of a single big turn: hextechanalytics says "Repeated efficient Showdowns, not one giant attack, are the finish"; riftbound.gg describes "controlling the pace of the match" via "a quiet early game" backed by a deep reactive-spell suite (Defy, Guardian Angel, Zhonya's Hourglass) — tempo/control-protection language, not combo. Both real sources also name **Stellacorn Herder** as a co-equal win-condition piece alongside Fervent, a card this entry's kit-only derivation never surfaces.

Legend ability: `When you choose a friendly unit, you may exhaust me and pay a rainbow rune to ready it. When you conquer, you may pay 1 Energy to ready me.` Champions analyzed here: **Irelia - Fervent** (Calm) — `Deflect. When you choose or ready me, give me +1 Might this turn.` **Irelia - Graceful** (Chaos) — `Your spells that choose me cost 1 Energy or a rainbow rune less.` (Real decks run Fervent almost exclusively — see the callout above; Graceful is not a genuine second lane in practice.)

The Legend ability is reactive to your own targeting — any time you "choose" (target) a friendly unit, you can pay to ready it. That converts self-targeting spells/abilities into extra actions rather than being a fixed-timing payoff, and it stacks with a second clause readying Irelia herself on every conquest. Fervent turns each choose-or-ready event into a stat gain (+1 Might), so repeatedly targeting her both readies her (Legend) and grows her (Fervent) in the same motion. Graceful discounts spells that choose her specifically, which reads on paper as the enabler for chaining several such triggers in one turn — but real decks don't build around Graceful at all (see callout), so treat this specific enabler as an unbuilt Tier 1 hypothesis rather than a real second lane.

Put together, the printed text alone suggests a combo-leaning identity: the payoff scales with how many "choose my own unit" effects you can chain onto one body in a single turn, not with how many units you field. A turn built around Graceful's discount could in principle let you fire multiple self-targeting spells at Fervent for an outsized single-turn swing — but real competitive Irelia decks don't play this way. Instead, real coverage describes a patient tempo/control shell: develop Fervent (and Stellacorn Herder, a real second key card this text-only derivation misses entirely) behind a deep bank of protection and countermagic, wait for the opponent to commit, then use cheap choose/move effects to ready and grow Fervent for incremental value across many turns. The real win condition is direct but not a combo burst: repeated readying lets the same unit contest or re-contest Showdowns turn after turn, protected by Deflect and reactive spells rather than won in one explosive turn — "repeated efficient Showdowns, not one giant attack," in the words of one real guide.
