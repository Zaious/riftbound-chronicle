### Shen - Eye of Twilight (Calm+Order)

> Tier 2-confirmed NOT CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — the control/defensive archetype identity holds up well across independent real sources, but the Champion analysis and win condition don't: neither Scourge of Shadows nor Leader of the Kinkou Order is the real default. A third Champion print this entry never analyzes, **Shen, Kinkou** (Origins Main Set, OGN-241, printed Shield 2 + Tank, no hold-conditional text at all), is confirmed "Chosen Champion" by hextechanalytics.com and CORE at 76.47% presence per riftDecks.com, while Scourge of Shadows (FLEX, 15.69%) and Leader of the Kinkou Order (FLEX, 9.8%) are both real-but-fringe. Real coverage describes the win condition as pure Tank-based damage redirection protecting a Hold, not either analyzed Champion's "exactly one other unit" draw/score trigger.

Legend ability: `Action: Exhaust: Give a friendly unit Tank this turn.` **Shen, Scourge of Shadows** (Calm): `When I hold, if there is exactly one other unit you control here, draw 1.` **Shen, Leader of the Kinkou Order** (Order): `Shield. When I hold, if there is exactly one other unit you control here, you score 1 point.`

Archetype: control, and unusually explicitly so — this reading is solidly corroborated by real coverage. hextechanalytics.com calls Shen a "defensive deck that controls where combat damage goes," and a separate guide describes the Legend as pure "defensive control: protect your best unit, hold your battlefields, and grind the game out rather than race to end it quickly" (skillshotzgaming.com). The Legend ability is a proactive protective tool (an Action, player-timed, not conditional) that grants Tank to shield a chosen unit from being finished off — defensive utility, not a scoring or tempo trigger in itself, and this part of the read matches real play closely: "How Shen Wins" (hextechanalytics.com) is described entirely in terms of directing combat damage onto a durable Tank unit to preserve the rest of the board and protect a Hold, with no mention of either analyzed Champion's hold-trigger text. **In real decks, neither Scourge of Shadows nor Leader of the Kinkou Order is the Champion that matters.** The actual default is **Shen, Kinkou** — an Origins-set print (OGN-241) this entry never considered, a plain Reaction unit with printed Shield 2 and Tank and no "exactly one other unit" condition at all — confirmed "Chosen Champion" by hextechanalytics.com and run CHOSEN in a real Riftbound Showdown Series Germany tournament list (2026-08-15). riftDecks.com's play-rate data confirms the gap: Shen, Kinkou is CORE at 76.47% presence; Scourge of Shadows sits at FLEX 15.69% (though it does slightly outperform when included, 1.32x); Leader of the Kinkou Order is FLEX at just 9.8% presence and underperforms (0.80x). The "exactly one other unit" specificity this entry treats as the deck's defining constraint belongs to two Champions real decks mostly don't run.

Core unfolding: early game is about getting Shen and exactly one partner to a battlefield and sitting there, using the Legend's Tank grant to protect whichever of the two is more exposed to being finished in combat — a plausible read of the printed text, but real decks generally build around a wider defensive unit base (Mournful Witness, Mutated Mouser, Stalwart Poro, Affectionate Poro all CORE/FLEX per riftDecks.com) rather than a strict two-unit garrison, since their actual Champion (Kinkou) carries no such restriction. Once established, real decks use Shen's Tank grant to redirect damage onto whichever unit can best absorb it (often Shen, Kinkou himself, or a dedicated Tank/Shield body), preserving card-draw pieces like Affectionate Poro and the overall Hold rather than chasing either analyzed Champion's hold-trigger payoff.

Win condition: the text-only read is holding uncontested, converted directly into points by Leader of the Kinkou Order or into card advantage by Scourge of Shadows. Real coverage instead frames the win condition as attrition through damage-redirection: outlast the opponent by keeping key units alive turn after turn via Tank, converting a defended Hold into steady value (card draw off surviving units, a slow scoreboard lead) rather than either Champion's specific hold-trigger text doing the scoring.
