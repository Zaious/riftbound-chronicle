### Nasus - Curator of the Sands (Calm+Mind)

Legend ability: `When you play a unit, gear, or activated ability with Energy cost 7 or more, you may exhaust me to ready up to 2 runes.` **Nasus, Ascended** (Calm): `Deflect 2. Empower 8 Energy. Empowered: When I conquer, you score 1 point.` **Nasus, Guardian of Knowledge** (Mind): `Once each turn, when an enemy unit here dies, channel 1 rune exhausted.`

Everything about this Legend gates behind reaching high Energy costs — the Legend ability itself only triggers off playing something at 7+ Energy, and its payoff (ready up to 2 runes) is a refund that exists purely to let the deck afford another expensive play sooner. Ascended pushes the threshold even further: Empower costs a full 8 Energy to activate, and only once Empowered does conquering start scoring an extra point directly — an explicit late-game payoff that has no relevance until the deck has already spent multiple turns ramping toward it. Guardian of Knowledge is passively attritional rather than proactive: once each turn, an enemy unit dying at its Battlefield channels a rune, which rewards a deck that's actually fighting and winning exchanges at a held point over multiple turns, not one racing to end the game quickly. There's no ambiguity here — this is control built around a genuinely high cost floor, using its own ramp to keep affording the next huge play rather than ever committing early. Early turns are about surviving and setting up runes/board presence cheaply enough to reach the 7-8 cost plays; once the engine is live, each expensive play refunds runes that fund the next one, letting the deck chain top-end pressure turn after turn instead of running dry. The close is explicit: Ascended converts the game's own win condition directly — conquering a Battlefield while Empowered scores an extra point on top of the normal one — so this deck's engine doesn't just indirectly support holding ground, it has a card that literally accelerates the score once its very expensive setup cost has been paid.
