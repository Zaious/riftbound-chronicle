### Jayce, Defender of Tomorrow (Mind+Body)

> Tier 2-confirmed CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — the combo/single-turn-swing win condition is corroborated by two independent real sources describing a gear-sequencing/combo plan that wants time to assemble; real tournament placements (Top 12 and Top 5 finishes) confirm this is a functioning, played archetype rather than a purely theoretical one. One blog source's claim that Jayce is "Mind domain only" was checked against two independent card databases, both confirming Mind+Body as stated here — that blog appears to be the one in error, not this entry.
>
> Follow-up 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — a source-data pipeline bug (fixed in `extract_legend_packets.py`) meant this entry never considered Jayce, Man of Progress (Mind, SFD-084/221, reprinted VEN-175/166), a real second Mind print. Real data (riftDecks.com, accessed 2026-08-18) shows Man of Progress is played about as often as Brilliant Inventor in Jayce's own decks (both FLEX, 28.04% presence, 1.0 average copies) but clearly outperforms it in top-cut rate (1.04x, above average, vs. Brilliant Inventor's 0.80x, well below average) — despite this entry's whole combo derivation being built around Brilliant Inventor without knowing Man of Progress existed. hextechanalytics.com's current guide (accessed 2026-08-18, citing a 2026-08-15 Top Shelf Masters 5th-place list) frames the choice among all three Champion prints as a live, unresolved split between "Gear cheating" (Man of Progress), "readying" (Brilliant Inventor), and "combat flexibility" (Hammer in Hand) — not a settled combo centered on Brilliant Inventor the way this entry presents it.

Legend ability: `Empower for 2 Energy and two rainbow runes (use only if not Empowered). 1 Energy, Exhaust: Ready a gear. Empowered: 1 Energy, Exhaust: Ready 2 gear.` Champions: **Jayce, Brilliant Inventor** (Mind) — `When you play me or the first time you play a non-token gear each turn, you may ready something besides me that's exhausted.` **Jayce, Hammer in Hand** (Body) — `When I become ready, choose one to give me this turn — Assault 2 / Deflect 2 / Ganking.`

The Legend ability is a paid, repeatable readying engine for gear, upgradeable via Empower into readying two gear per activation instead of one — no opponent-dependent condition, just resources spent to generate extra "ready" events. Brilliant Inventor extends this beyond gear: playing a gear (the first each turn) or playing Jayce himself lets you ready something else that's exhausted, broadening the readying pool. Hammer in Hand is where this converges into a combo shape — every time Jayce becomes ready, by any means, he gets to choose a fresh combat keyword for the turn: Assault 2 to hit harder, Deflect 2 to survive interaction, or Ganking to reposition to another battlefield. That means each readying event isn't just "he can act again," it's a reconfigurable power-up each time.

Structurally, this rewards stacking multiple readying triggers into one turn — Empowered gear-readying plus Brilliant Inventor's own-turn readying — so Jayce can re-enter combat, defend safely, or Gank to a new location more than once before the turn ends, a compounding single-turn swing rather than a slow grind or a wide board. Early game wants a gear on board early to feed Brilliant Inventor's trigger and to give the Legend ability something to ready. Once running, Empower unlocks the double-gear-ready, and every resulting readying (of gear or of Jayce via Brilliant Inventor) triggers Hammer in Hand's keyword choice again. The close-out is direct: a readied Jayce can contest or move to contest multiple battlefields within a single turn, so the win condition is winning Showdowns outright by re-engaging combat repeatedly in one turn, with Ganking letting each fresh readying attack a different location rather than just repeating the same fight.

*(Tier 2 follow-up correction: Brilliant Inventor is not this Legend's only real Mind-domain option, and real play doesn't treat it as the clearly dominant one — Jayce, Man of Progress (Mind, "When you play me, you may kill a friendly gear. If you do, you may play a gear with Energy cost no more than 7 from hand this turn, ignoring its Energy cost.") is played about equally often and performs better in real results. Man of Progress represents a different plan than the readying-combo described above: it cheats an expensive Gear into play immediately off a cheaper one, rather than stacking readying triggers for a multi-attack turn. Treat the combo shape above as one of two real, comparably-supported Mind-side routes, not the default.)*
