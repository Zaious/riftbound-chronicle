### Lee Sin - Blind Monk (Calm+Body)

Legend ability: `1 Energy, Exhaust: Buff a friendly unit. (If it doesn't have a buff, it gets a +1 might buff.)` **Lee Sin - Ascetic** (Calm): `Shield. Exhaust: Buff me. (+1 might buff.) I can have any number of buffs.` **Lee Sin - Centered** (Body): `Accelerate. Other buffed friendly units at my battlefield have +2 might.`

**Archetype**: Control/midrange, with the two Champions splitting into different shapes of the same lane. The Legend ability is a cheap, player-initiated, repeatable buff generator with no combat or turn-phase gate — it rewards sustained, turn-over-turn investment rather than a burst or a race. Ascetic exploits the fact that most units cap at one buff instance: she alone "can have any number of buffs," turning repeated activations (from herself or the Legend) into an unbounded, slow-building single threat — structurally a patient, compounding-over-many-turns plan. Centered instead rewards width: her static bonus (+2 Might to any buffed unit at her battlefield) means several modestly-buffed units are better than one heavily stacked one, alongside an Accelerate-driven early presence.

**Core unfolding**: Early game wants a unit at a contested battlefield receiving cheap, repeated buffs from the Legend ability. With Ascetic, investment concentrates onto her specifically over several turns, building toward one outsized body. With Centered, the deck instead spreads buffs across multiple units to convert Centered's flat +2 into a group-wide advantage. Either way, the payoff is stat superiority accrued gradually, not a single turn's swing — this closes out by holding a battlefield with units that have become disproportionately strong relative to their printed cost.

**Engine to points**: Pure control/attrition — Might inflation converts into winning, then holding, Showdowns turn after turn. Nothing here generates cards or independent resources; the entire plan is stat accumulation in service of ground control.
