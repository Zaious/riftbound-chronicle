### Lux - Lady of Luminosity (Mind+Order)

> Tier 2-confirmed NOT CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — the text-derived "control/midrange spell-payoff shell" below describes a real, once-competitive build (real coverage confirms Lux "looked sort of competitive... playing a pure control strategy" through an early regional), but it is **not** what real coverage currently treats as this Legend's defining archetype. The community-defining Lux deck is instead described as one of the game's only true combo decks: an infinite-Energy loop (Forge of the Future → Ekko, Recurrent → Shadow's Call → Sacrifice) that repeatedly casts Time Warp to hold a Battlefield to 8 points. Neither Crownguard's nor Illuminated's printed ability is part of that loop at all — real data shows Illuminated is close to unplayed in practice (roughly 1 deck a month, and even that copy was paired with a different Legend, not Lux). Treat the paragraph below as a valid demonstration of deriving identity from primary text — it's accurate to what the Legend+Champion kit itself says — but not as a description of what a "build me a Lux deck" request should actually produce today; that's the infinite-combo shell, built from cards outside this kit entirely.

Legend ability: `When you play a spell that costs 5 Energy or more, draw 1.` **Lux, Crownguard** (Order): `Exhaust: Reaction — Add 2 Energy. Spend this Energy only to play spells.` **Lux - Illuminated** (Mind): `When you play a spell that costs 5 Energy or more, give me +3 Might this turn.`

The Legend trigger fires immediately on-play, not end-of-turn or conditionally-delayed, but it's gated behind a real cost threshold (5+ Energy) rather than any spell — so the deck isn't rewarded for flooding cheap interaction, it's rewarded for actually landing its expensive spells and getting paid the instant it does. Crownguard's Reaction adds Energy earmarked only for spells, at instant speed, which reads as resource-smoothing to make sure that 5-cost threshold is reachable on curve or held up defensively rather than only on a dedicated big-spell turn. Illuminated redirects the same trigger into an immediate +3 might on Lux herself, converting card advantage into combat relevance in the same turn. Read purely from this text, this looks like a control/midrange spell-payoff shell — the plan would be to reach the point where each big spell is efficient (replaces itself via draw) while Crownguard keeps Energy available reactively. **This is exactly the kind of case the "note where a real deck doesn't build around the Legend's own kit" caveat exists for**: real competitive Lux is a combo deck built on cards (Forge of the Future, Ekko - Recurrent, Shadow's Call, Sacrifice, Time Warp) that don't appear anywhere in this Legend+Champion text, and the printed Crownguard/Illuminated triggers are largely incidental to it.
