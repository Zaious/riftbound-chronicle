### Darius - Hand of Noxus (Fury+Order)

> Tier 2-confirmed 2026-08-17, re-verified with real citations 2026-08-17 (`references/verification-log.md`) — real coverage does describe this deck with tempo-forward language (riftbound.gg: "develop a ton of tempo") and identifies `OGN-242` (Baited Hook) as the deck's build-around core piece — that part is solidly confirmed with a real, checkable citation. The specific framing "tempo, not pure aggro" is softer than the label the entry originally used, though: a second real, independent source (RiftStorm.gg) describes the exact same deck as "Fury/Order beatdown" with "aggressive bodies," using aggro-coded language directly. Real community labeling for this deck isn't a clean single consensus between "tempo" and "aggro" — report both rather than pick one as the settled real label.

Legend ability: `Exhaust: Reaction, Legion — Add 1 Energy. (Abilities that add resources can't be reacted to. Get the effect if you've played a card this turn.)`
Darius - Trifarian (Fury): `When you play your second card in a turn, give me +2 Might this turn and ready me.`
Darius - Executioner (Order): `Legion — When you play me, ready me. (Get the effect if you've played another card this turn) Other friendly units have +1 Might here.`

**Archetype:** Tempo — the real community label, not this book's own aggro/control/combo three-way split. All three pieces of this kit key off the same immediate, same-turn condition — playing a second card in a turn (Legion) — and every payoff resolves instantly, not on a delay: extra energy (Legend), a might buff plus a free ready (Trifarian), or entering combat-ready plus an anthem (Executioner). A pure text-structural read points straight at "aggro" (no reactive gate, no trash requirement, no end-of-turn timer), but real practice specifically distinguishes this from pure aggro: the Legion condition is a resource-sequencing engine — pushing a spell, unit ability, or combat trick past what the opponent expects on a given turn — more than it's a race to deal damage first. Legion is also strongest specifically early (a single extra rune matters far more at 3-4 total runes than at 7-8), which frames this as a deck that wants to win the *early* tempo battle through efficient sequencing, not sustain aggression indefinitely.

**Core unfolding:** Early game wants a curve stacked with cheap, efficient second plays so that Legion triggers reliably from turn 1 or 2 onward — the Legend's extra energy directly helps fund that second card in the first place, making the loop partially self-sustaining, and real lists build specifically around a core sequencing piece (community-cited: OGN-242) plus disposable/situational cards recyclable into stronger ones. Once it's online, hitting Legion in a turn gives Trifarian +2 might and readies him, meaning he can attack, come back up, and threaten to attack or defend again the same cycle; Executioner instead enters already ready and buffs every friendly unit at his battlefield by +1 might, stacking board-wide pressure at a single point of contest. Both scale purely with how consistently the deck can hit two-plus cards a turn, and real practice confirms this pays off hardest specifically in the early turns, not as a sustained late-game plan.

**Win condition:** Wins Showdowns outright through repeated readies and stat pushes, funded by consistently overloaded early turns — there's no hold-scoring or card-engine text anywhere in this kit, so the plan is forcing and winning individual Showdowns while the Legion-timing advantage is at its strongest, not grinding out a long game.
