### Ambessa - Matriarch of War (Body+Order)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — the mechanical read and Champion identities hold up, and Ambessa, The Wolf is confirmed the real default Chosen Champion (CORE, 82.76% presence vs. Respected and Feared's FLEX 36.78%, though Feared performs slightly better per-inclusion). But "nothing here is reactive or held in reserve... not a holding or card-advantage plan" is contradicted by two independent real sources: a Cardsrealm kit review compares the archetype to "an 'Irelia' style deck" and calls it "the slower identity focused on controlling the board, removing threats, and accumulating resources," and hextechanalytics's own late-game section says the plan is to "hold one battlefield while threatening the other, rather than rebuilding the same all-in turn." Real results are also thin so far — riftbound.gg (updated Aug 14) says Ambessa is "struggling early in Vendetta" with just a single City Challenge Top 8, and she sits at Tier 4 on the current tier list — a fresh-Legend caveat in the same family as Renekton/Akali.

Legend ability: `When you empower something else, empower me. (I become Empowered if I'm not already.) Disempower me, a rainbow rune, Exhaust: Ready a unit.`
Ambessa, The Wolf (Body): `Empower 3 Energy, a Body rune. Empowered: I have +3 Might and can't be dealt damage unless I'm in combat.`
Ambessa, Respected and Feared (Order): `Empower 1 Energy, two Order runes. Empowered: I have Assault 2. Empowered: When I attack, kill an enemy unit here with less Might than me.`

**Archetype:** Aggro. Both Champions are themselves Empower payoffs that turn a paid-up-front investment into a hard-to-answer attacker: Feared gets Assault 2 plus an on-attack execute against anything smaller, Wolf gets +3 might and immunity to non-combat removal. Empowering either one also empowers the Legend (a free chain), which can then be spent to ready a separate unit — an extra attacker or blocker on demand. Nothing here is reactive or held in reserve; the whole kit is about paying a cost this turn to push through combat this turn or next.

**Core unfolding:** Early game wants the chosen Champion down and Empowered as soon as resources allow, since the payoff is immediate combat dominance rather than a delayed engine — Feared's on-attack kill clears blockers outright, Wolf's damage immunity outside combat makes him nearly impossible to remove before he swings. Once that first Empower lands, it auto-triggers the Legend's own Empower, which can be cashed in to ready a second unit — effectively banking an extra attack or defense each turn once the chain is running, so board presence compounds turn-over-turn rather than needing a single big payoff. Closing stages look like repeatedly attacking into blockers Feared can simply kill, or trading into a Wolf the opponent can't answer outside of combat, backed by an extra readied unit most turns.

**Win condition:** Wins Showdowns outright via stat and removal advantage — killing smaller blockers on attack, or fielding a near-unkillable body. This text-derived reading holds up as far as it goes, but real coverage describes a somewhat slower, more patient version of it than "not a holding plan" implies — see the Tier 2 callout above: real guides frame the late game as holding one battlefield while pressuring a second, and one pre-launch review likened the whole shape to an "Irelia"-style single-unit resource investment rather than a pure push-through-now aggro plan.
