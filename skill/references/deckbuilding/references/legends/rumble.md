### Rumble - Mechanized Menace (Fury+Mind)

> Tier 2-confirmed PARTIALLY CONFIRMED 2026-08-18 (`references/verification-log.md`) — real, but not a Champion-choice fork the way this entry originally framed it. Tracked competitive decks run Hotheaded near-universally (~95% presence) *and* Scrapper in over half of decks (~53%) — the two figures sum well past 100%, meaning most winning decks play **both Champions together** as one synergistic package, not a fork where a deckbuilder commits to one. A real deck guide for the official pre-built product frames them explicitly as complementary: Hotheaded "handles the conquest side while Scrapper handles the hold... between the two of them, the deck has an answer for both phases of the game." A separate, Scrapper-only "pure hold" archetype does have its own real coverage — but that coverage is explicit that it's one of the weakest decks in the game, not a comparably-viable alternative lane.

Legend ability: `Your Mechs have Shield. (+1 might while they're defenders.)` **Rumble - Hotheaded** (Fury): `Your Mechs each have Assault. When I conquer, you may recycle another friendly unit to play a Mech from your trash. Reduce its Energy cost by the Might of the unit you recycled.` **Rumble - Scrapper** (Mind): `Your Mechs have +1 might (including me). When I hold, play a 3 might Mech unit token to your base.`

Archetype: hybrid — and real play backs the "hybrid" label better than a "split" one. The Legend's own ability is static and defensive by default — Shield only boosts Mechs while defending, tone-setting a tribe that's naturally good at holding ground. Hotheaded's on-conquer recursion and Scrapper's on-hold token generation read as pulling toward offense and defense respectively, but the dominant real pattern is running both in the same 40-card deck: Hotheaded's Assault keyword and trash recursion push the attack, Scrapper's flat +1 Might buff and hold-triggered tokens support holding what's already been taken — sequential phases of one plan (conquer, then reinforce and hold), not two competing ones.

**Core unfolding:** the deck goes wide with buffed, Shielded Mechs from the start; Hotheaded-style conquest pressure opens battlefields and recurs spent units into cheaper reinforcements, while Scrapper's presence (usually alongside Hotheaded, not instead of him) keeps generating free bodies once a position is held, so the transition from taking ground to keeping it doesn't require switching decks. A dedicated Scrapper-only "Holding Rumble" build exists in real coverage but is explicitly described there as struggling against faster, more consistent meta decks — real, but a weak fallback rather than a coequal archetype.

**Win condition:** both phases matter in the same deck — Hotheaded's recursion snowballs conquest, Scrapper's hold-trigger generates free reinforcement to keep what's taken, and most competitive lists lean on both rather than picking one lane.
